## Core

**E08.06** Credit costs are cyclical and mean-revert; a trough in NPAs means provisions are minimal and future
slippages are drawn from an unseasoned, fast-grown book. Through-cycle: large private bank ~0.7–1.0%; PSU bank
~1.0–1.5%; MFI ~2.5–4% with event spikes — versus FY26 actuals well below these.

**E08.07** Revenue per employee ≈ 1.03/0.96 = +7.3%. Interpretations: (a) AI-driven productivity — the company
delivers the same work with fewer people and keeps the margin; (b) AI deflation — pricing falls with headcount
and the productivity accrues to clients. The settling numbers: revenue per employee *and* EBIT margin over the
next four quarters; if margins hold as headcount falls, (a); if revenue growth turns negative on existing
contracts, (b).

**E08.08** Track: decorative volume growth vs industry; market share (independent, quarterly); gross margin vs
input basket; A&P and dealer incentives as % of sales; dealer count and tinting-machine adds vs Birla Opus's;
price realisation. Breach threshold: two consecutive years of share loss > 2 pp *with* gross-margin compression
> 200 bps not explained by inputs — share being defended with margin, i.e. the pricing-power test failing.

**E08.09** Plant supplies 60% of US EBITDA = 27% of total EBITDA. Existing products erode ~8%/yr → after two years
~85% of that stream remains (−4 pp of total); launches lost (say 10–15% of US growth) → a further 3–5 pp. EBITDA at
risk ~7–9% over two years, more if import alert follows. Demand: the FDA letter's observations, remediation
timeline, share of pending ANDAs filed from the plant, and alternate-site transfer plans.

**E08.10** Visibility ≈ 2,50,000/35,000 ≈ **7 years**. A backlog converts at the pace of engines, components and
skilled assembly (Tejas Mk1A slipped on GE engine supply); large contracts have staggered deliveries starting
years out (Prachand from FY29); and MoD payment and acceptance cycles lag. The constraint is execution capacity,
not orders.

**E08.11** Retail > wholesale means dealer inventory is *falling* (a healthy sign after a build-up) — OEMs will
restock, so wholesale growth should follow. Pull next: FADA's dealer-inventory days by segment and the discount
levels reported by dealers/brokers.

**E08.12** Demand: 650 × 0.72 = 468 MT → × 1.07³ = 573 MT in FY28; capacity 650 + 165 = 815 MT → utilisation
**~70%**, down slightly — prices soften in regions where additions concentrate (South, East), leaders push volumes,
EBITDA/tonne compresses, and the weaker players become acquisition targets.

**E08.13** OMC: marketing losses on diesel/petrol (negative margins per litre) offset partly by refining
gains and inventory gains; PAT falls sharply. Upstream: realisation rises but the windfall/special excise duty
caps the net gain; PAT rises moderately. CGD: higher spot-LNG share in gas cost with CNG prices sticky → margin per
scm compresses; volume growth slows if CNG loses its price advantage.

**E08.14** The business is growing (pre-sales up); revenue reflects old projects completing. Replace with
pre-sales (value and area), collections, and operating cash flow / net debt.

**E08.15** Per 100 mn subscribers: Airtel: 264 × 15% × 12 × 100 mn × 0.9 = **₹4,277 Cr** EBITDA; Vi: 177 × 15% × 12 ×
100 mn × 0.9 = **₹2,867 Cr**. Vi's equity is a deep out-of-the-money call on the enterprise (liabilities ≫ EV):
a rupee of EBITDA raises asset value against a fixed strike, so the percentage change in equity is far larger
than for Airtel, whose equity is well in the money.

**E08.16** Lines: cash-equity transaction fees; equity-derivative transaction fees (index options and futures,
stock derivatives); currency/commodity/other segments; clearing and margin float income; listing, data, index
licensing and co-location. Scenarios: SEBI fee caps or product curbs on derivatives; volume regime shifts;
interoperability/competition (BSE); technology incidents. Cross-check: global exchange EV/EBITDA and P/E
(e.g., CME, ICE, HKEX, B3 — verify) adjusted for growth and regulatory risk.

**E08.17** Agri: monsoon and rural incomes (consumer/autos playbooks — IMD, VAHAN tractors); industrial: private
capex and PMI/IIP (industrials playbook); solar: PM-KUSUM budget allocations, state DISCOM/agency finances and
ALMM/module prices (energy playbook).
