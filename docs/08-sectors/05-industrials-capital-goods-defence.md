# 08.5 · Industrials, capital goods, defence, railways & EMS

> **Sector playbook.** The capital-goods cycle is India's 2020s story: record government capex (₹12.2 lakh Cr
> proposed for FY27), a defence-indigenisation push with order books that run to a decade of revenue, railways
> capex of ₹2.9 lakh Cr, and a PLI-driven electronics manufacturing (EMS) boom. Order books are the vocabulary;
> execution, working capital and margins are the analysis. This is also Kaveri Pumps' home sector.

**Time:** ~80 min · **Prerequisites:** [05.4 The capital cycle](../05-business-analysis/04-capital-cycle-and-competition.md), [04.4 Working capital](../04-financial-analysis/04-working-capital-and-cash-conversion.md)

---

## 1. How the industry makes money

| Segment | Model | EBITDA margin | Working capital | Order visibility | Customer |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **Engineered products** (pumps, motors, compressors, valves, bearings, abrasives, cables) | Standard products via dealers/OEMs; some project sales | 12–20% | 60–100 days of NWC | Short cycle; dealer restocking | Fragmented industrial/agri; some government |
| **Capital equipment** (turbines, transformers, switchgear, machine tools, cranes) | Engineered-to-order; project-linked | 10–18% | High (advances offset) | Order book 1–3 years | Utilities, industry, government |
| **EPC / infrastructure** (L&T-type; roads, power T&D, water, buildings) | Fixed-price/lump-sum contracts; execution risk | 8–12% | Very high (retention, unbilled, BGs) | Order book 2–4 years; book-to-bill | Government, PSUs, private |
| **Defence** (aircraft, ships, missiles, electronics, ammunition) | Cost-plus/nominated or competitive; long-gestation; DPSUs dominate | 20–30% (DPSUs) / 10–20% (private) | Customer advances make NWC low or negative for DPSUs; receivables from MoD can lag | Order book 3–10 years of revenue (HAL ~₹2.5 lakh Cr — [Defence Standard](https://defencestandard.com/guides/indian-defence-industry/)) | Ministry of Defence; exports |
| **Railways** (wagons, coaches, locomotives, signalling, track) | Tender-based; Indian Railways as near-monopsony | 8–15% | Moderate; payment discipline good | Order book 2–4 years; budget-driven | Indian Railways, metros |
| **EMS** (electronics manufacturing services — phones, TVs, appliances, wearables, PCBs) | Contract manufacturing for brands; PLI-linked | 3–5% (assembly) to 8–12% (components, design) | Moderate; customer-funded | Customer programmes; PLI targets | Global and Indian brands |

Government is the customer in defence, railways, T&D and most EPC — payment cycles, budget allocations and
policy (indigenisation lists, PLI, ECMS) drive the sector as much as demand does ([05.2 India notes](../05-business-analysis/02-industry-analysis.md)).

## 2. The cycle as of September 2026

- **Public capex**: FY27 budget capex ₹12.2 lakh Cr ([IBEF](https://www.ibef.org/news/us-135-6-billion-public-capex-proposed-in-fy27));
  railways capex ₹2.93 lakh Cr, ~39% spent by July ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/budget/news/indian-railways-record-capex-fy27-high-speed-rail-freight-corridor-126020100735_1.html)).
  The question for the cycle is private capex: has it followed public capex (power, cement, steel, EMS say
  yes; broad-based manufacturing less clearly)?
- **Defence**: capital outlay ₹2.2 lakh Cr for FY27 (+22%), ₹1.39 lakh Cr for domestic procurement
  ([KPMG](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/in/pdf/2026/02/aerospace-and-defence-pov-union-budget-2026-27.pdf));
  HAL's 97 additional Tejas Mk1A (₹62,000 Cr) and 156 Prachand helicopters (₹62,800 Cr); execution — engine
  supply from GE, delivery slippage — is the constraint, not orders.
- **EMS**: Dixon and peers reached ~28% of India's smartphone assembly in FY26; the ₹40,000 Cr Electronics
  Components Manufacturing Scheme (ECMS) shifts the incentive from assembly to components
  ([Techovedas](https://techovedas.com/budget-2026-rs-40000-cr-ecms-surge-rockets-ems-stocks-dixon-kaynes-hit-7/)).
  Assembly margins are thin and PLI-dependent; the value question is whether component/design capabilities
  arrive before PLI expires.
- **Capital-cycle warning** ([05.4](../05-business-analysis/04-capital-cycle-and-competition.md)): defence and
  railway stocks re-rated 3–10x in 2022–24 on order-book narratives, corrected in 2024–25, and remain priced for
  execution that has historically slipped. Solar-equipment capacity (modules, cells) has been added far faster
  than demand — the classic overshoot.

## 3. KPIs

| KPI | Definition | Notes |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **Order book / order inflow / book-to-bill** | Unexecuted orders; new orders in the period; inflow ÷ revenue | Book-to-bill > 1 = growing backlog; check the *executable* order book (some orders are framework/optional) |
| **Order book / revenue (years of visibility)** | | 2–4x normal for EPC; 5–10x for DPSUs — but conversion is slow |
| **Execution / conversion rate** | Revenue ÷ opening order book | Historical rate predicts revenue better than management guidance |
| **Margin by contract type** | Fixed-price vs cost-plus; segment margins | Fixed-price EPC margins fall when input costs rise mid-contract (2021–22 steel) |
| **Working capital**: NWC days, unbilled revenue, retention money, customer advances, mobilisation advances, BGs outstanding | Notes; presentations | The sector's cash-flow killer; Kaveri's solar receivables in miniature |
| **CFO/EBITDA over 3 years** | | EPC companies often show < 50% |
| **Capacity utilisation** | For product companies | Kaveri: pumps 78%, motors 57% |
| **PLI / incentive income** | Other operating income | Policy-dependent; separate from core margins |
| **Export share** | | Defence exports policy target; engineering exports to US/EU |

## 4. What drives earnings and multiples

Government budget and payment discipline; private capex revival; commodity input costs (steel, copper —
Kaveri's gross-margin story); policy (PLI, ECMS, indigenisation lists, Make-in-India procurement preference,
"Atmanirbhar"); interest rates (financing for buyers); execution capability (skilled labour, engines, components);
exports. Multiples: product companies with strong franchises (Cummins, ABB, Siemens, Polycab, Havells) 40–60x at
the cycle peak; EPC 20–30x; DPSUs re-rated from 10–15x to 30–50x in 2022–24, then partially corrected; EMS 60–100x
at peak on growth narratives. The sector's multiples embed cycle-peak margins and order-book conversion that the
capital-cycle framework says to fade.

## 5. Accounting quirks

Percentage-of-completion revenue on contracts (Ind AS 115 over-time recognition): unbilled revenue/contract assets,
cost-to-complete estimates that can be optimistic; retention money in receivables; customer advances as contract
liabilities (negative working capital for DPSUs is *customer money*, not efficiency); liquidated damages provisions;
warranty provisions; PLI income recognition timing; joint ventures and consortium accounting in EPC; CWIP ageing for
capacity builds (Kaveri's Hosur); export incentives.

## 6. Red flags

Order book growing while execution rate falls; unbilled revenue and retention rising faster than revenue; margins
held by capitalising costs or under-provisioning liquidated damages; working-capital borrowings funding receivables
from state entities (Kaveri); order announcements that never appear in revenue; related-party subcontracting; a
company "diversifying" into defence/EV/solar to catch a narrative; PLI-dependent margins presented as structural;
promoter pledges rising with the stock (the 2023–24 small-cap defence/railway rally saw many).

## 7. How to value

Product companies: DCF with capacity/utilisation and margin drivers, cross-checked with P/E vs growth and ROCE
(Kaveri's [reference valuation](../appendix/running-example/kaveri-valuation.md) is the template). EPC/defence:
order-book conversion DCF (revenue = executable backlog × conversion + new orders × win rate), margin by contract
type, explicit working-capital build, and a scenario for payment delays; P/E only with the cycle stage stated.
EMS: DCF with PLI expiry and margin-mix drivers; EV/Sales sanity check against terminal margins
([07.4](../07-special-valuation/04-high-growth-and-loss-making.md) logic applies).

## 9. The 10-question sector checklist

1. Order book: size, executable portion, ageing, and historical conversion rate?
2. Margin by contract type, and exposure to input-cost inflation on fixed-price work?
3. Working capital: unbilled, retention, advances, BGs, receivables from government entities — trend?
4. CFO/EBITDA over three years?
5. Customer: which budget line funds this, and what is the payment record?
6. Policy dependence: PLI/ECMS/indigenisation — what happens when it changes?
7. Capacity: utilisation, capex plans, and industry-wide capacity announcements (the capital cycle)?
8. Execution constraints: components, engines, skilled labour, land?
9. Governance: promoter pledges, related-party subcontracting, announcements vs delivery?
10. What conversion rate and margin does the multiple imply, and how does that compare with history?
