# 08.4 · Pharma & healthcare

> **Sector playbook.** Indian pharma is several businesses wearing one label: a domestic branded-generics market
> with FMCG-like economics, a US generics export business that swings with price erosion and FDA inspections, a
> fast-growing contract-manufacturing (CDMO) segment riding "China+1", an API industry, and — separately — hospitals
> and diagnostics with their own metrics. Analysing "a pharma company" means first decomposing it into these
> segments and applying the right lens to each.

**Time:** ~80 min · **Prerequisites:** [05.2 Industry analysis](../05-business-analysis/02-industry-analysis.md), [07.3 Cyclicals](../07-special-valuation/03-cyclicals-and-commodities.md)

## 1. How the industry makes money

| Segment | Model | Margin | Growth driver | Risk |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **Domestic formulations (branded generics)** | Prescription brands sold via medical reps to doctors; chronic (diabetes, cardiac) vs acute (antibiotics, pain) | EBITDA 25–35%; gross 65–75% | Volume + price (limited by DPCO on scheduled drugs) + new launches; chronic share rising | Price control (NLEM/DPCO); trade generics; regulatory changes to prescribing |
| **US generics** | ANDA approvals; launch on patent expiry; first-to-file (180-day exclusivity) for Para IV; price competition among 5–15 players per molecule | EBITDA 15–25% (volatile) | Patent cliff pipeline; complex generics/injectables/specialty where competition is thinner | Price erosion (high-single to double digits in 2017–22; low-single-digit to flat by 2024–26 for many, but still high in crowded molecules — [ICRA](https://www.bwhealthcareworld.com/article/pricing-pressures-regulatory-scrutiny-persisting-in-the-us-generics-pharma-industry-icra-438846)); USFDA compliance (Form 483s, warning letters, import alerts) |
| **Specialty / branded (US, EU)** | Own branded products (dermatology, ophthalmology, oncology) with sales forces | Can reach 30%+ once scaled; heavy upfront R&D and SG&A | Launches; label expansions | Trial failures; payer pushback |
| **CDMO / CRDMO** | Contract development and manufacturing for innovators (small molecules, peptides, biologics, ADCs) | EBITDA 25–35% at scale; lumpy | China+1 diversification; US BIOSECURE-type policies; innovator outsourcing | Customer concentration; project timing; capacity build-outs ahead of contracts |
| **APIs / intermediates** | Bulk drug manufacturing; commodity to complex | EBITDA 15–25%; cyclical | Regulatory approvals; China supply dynamics; PLI for KSMs | Chinese pricing; environmental compliance |
| **Hospitals** | Beds; case mix; occupancy; ARPOB | EBITDA 18–25% for mature networks | Bed additions; occupancy; ARPOB (case mix, international patients); insurance penetration | Regulatory price caps (pending proposals); doctor attrition; capex cycles |
| **Diagnostics** | Test volumes × realisation; B2B vs B2C; franchise collection centres | EBITDA 25–30% (down from 40%+ pre-2021 competition) | Volumes; premium tests; consolidation | Price competition from new entrants; test-price transparency |
| **Health insurance** | See [07.2](../07-special-valuation/02-insurers-amcs-exchanges.md) | | | |

## 3. What drives earnings and multiples

- **Domestic**: steady 8–12% market growth; chronic-heavy portfolios grow faster and command higher multiples;
  price control expansions (NLEM revisions) and the trade-generics push are the regulatory swing factors.
- **US**: the price-erosion cycle. After brutal erosion in 2017–22, pricing found a floor by 2024–26 as
  competitors exited and shortages emerged; margins recovered, then investors debated whether it lasts
  ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/industry/pharma/story/india-pharma-turns-to-specialty-drugs-as-generics-pricing-bite-deepens-510917-2026-01-14)).
  The other swing is **FDA compliance**: a warning letter or import alert on a major plant removes a year or more
  of launches — the single most common reason an Indian pharma stock halves.
- **Trade policy**: US tariff threats on pharma imports (2025–26) and any India–US trade deal outcomes; verify the
  current position, since it moves the whole export cohort.
- **CDMO**: the China+1 narrative drove multiples to 40–60x for the leaders (Divi's, Syngene, Laurus, Neuland,
  Piramal Pharma — verify); the cycle depends on innovator R&D budgets, biotech funding and policy (BIOSECURE).
- **Hospitals**: bed additions and ARPOB growth (8–12% a year) with high incremental margins; multiples of 20–30x
  EV/EBITDA for the leaders reflect the runway; risk is government price regulation.
- **Multiples**: domestic-heavy companies 25–35x; US-heavy generics 15–25x (cyclical); CDMO 35–60x; hospitals
  20–30x EV/EBITDA; diagnostics de-rated from 60x+ to 30–40x after 2021 competition. Sum-of-segments valuation is
  the norm for diversified companies.

## 4. Accounting quirks

R&D expensed (Ind AS 38 allows capitalisation of development only after technical/commercial feasibility — most
Indian pharma expenses it; compare R&D as % of sales); milestone and licensing income lumpy in other operating
revenue; inventory write-downs on failed launches; litigation provisions (US patent and antitrust cases —
contingent liabilities can be large); acquisition accounting for brand portfolios (intangibles amortised, often
"adjusted" out of EPS); Ind AS 116 for hospitals (leased buildings); hospitals' capital-work-in-progress for new
units and pre-opening losses.

## 5. Red flags

FDA inspection outcomes not disclosed promptly; a plant with repeated 483s supplying most US revenue; US revenue
guided up while ANDA approvals stall; domestic growth "in line with IPM" while chronic share falls; CDMO capex far
ahead of contracted revenue; related-party API sourcing; hospital ARPOB rising only through mix toward
international patients; receivables from government schemes (Ayushman Bharat) building at hospitals; diagnostics
volume growth from low-margin B2B while realisation falls; promoter actions (pledges, group companies in
unrelated businesses) — several mid-cap pharma governance episodes in 2018–24 are worth reading.

## 6. How to value

Sum of segments: domestic on a DCF or P/E with a consumer-like fade; US generics on normalised margins
([07.3](../07-special-valuation/03-cyclicals-and-commodities.md) logic — price erosion is the cycle) with an explicit
FDA-event scenario; specialty on risk-adjusted NPV of products; CDMO on contracted growth with capex and customer
concentration; hospitals on EV/EBITDA per bed and bed-addition DCF; diagnostics on P/E with competition scenario.
Always run a reverse DCF on CDMO and hospital names — those are where the market's growth expectations are most
demanding.

## 8. The 10-question sector checklist

1. Segment split: how much profit is domestic, US, CDMO, other — and which lens applies to each?
2. Domestic: growth vs IPM, chronic share, DPCO exposure?
3. US: price-erosion trend, launch pipeline, and the FDA status of every plant that supplies the US?
4. Trade policy: exposure to tariffs or trade deals?
5. R&D: % of sales, split by generics/specialty/biosimilars, expensed or capitalised?
6. CDMO: contracted revenue vs capex, customer concentration, phase mix?
7. Hospitals: occupancy, ARPOB, bed pipeline, regulatory price-cap risk?
8. Litigation and contingent liabilities (US patent/antitrust)?
9. Governance: promoter group, related-party manufacturing, past regulatory actions?
10. What does the multiple imply for growth and margins in each segment, and which segment carries the risk?
