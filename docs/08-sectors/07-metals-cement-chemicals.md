# 08.7 · Metals, cement & chemicals

> **Sector playbook.** Three capital-intensive, cyclical industries where the analytical toolkit of
> [07.3](../07-special-valuation/03-cyclicals-and-commodities.md) — normalised margins, EV/tonne, cost curves,
> the capital cycle — does most of the work. Steel is fighting Chinese and Vietnamese imports behind a safeguard
> duty; cement is adding 160–170 million tonnes of capacity in three years; chemicals are working through the
> hangover of the 2021–23 capex boom. As of September 2026.

**Time:** ~75 min · **Prerequisites:** [07.3 Cyclicals & commodities](../07-special-valuation/03-cyclicals-and-commodities.md), [05.4 The capital cycle](../05-business-analysis/04-capital-cycle-and-competition.md)

---

## 1. Steel

**How it makes money.** Convert iron ore + coking coal (blast furnace) or scrap/DRI + power (electric arc) into
steel; sell at a domestic price linked to the import-parity price (global price + freight + duties). Margin =
realisation − raw-material and conversion cost; captive iron ore (Tata Steel, SAIL, JSW partially) and coking
coal access define the cost-curve position. Flat products (auto, appliances, pipes) vs long products
(construction); value-added grades earn premiums.

| KPI | Notes |
|:--|:--|
| Volumes (MT) and capacity; utilisation | Capacity ~180 MT (India) heading to 300 MT by 2030 per government targets — verify; the capital cycle in one number |
| Realisation per tonne; EBITDA per tonne | The cycle variables; FY22 super-cycle ₹15–20k/t at the leaders, FY24 trough ₹5–8k/t |
| Raw-material cost: iron ore (NMDC prices), coking coal (Australian benchmark), captive share | Coking coal is the imported, volatile input |
| Imports and safeguard duty | India imposed a three-year safeguard duty on flat steel imports (2025), with a tapered structure proposed by DGTR ([Metalbook](https://www.metalbook.com/blogs/indian-steelmakers-seek-safeguard-duties-in/); [Business Standard, Aug-2026](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/industry/news/safeguard-duty-higher-prices-to-keep-steel-makers-profitability-steady-126080601814_1.html)); the duty level is the domestic-price floor |
| Exports and CBAM | Europe's carbon border adjustment (from 2026) raises the cost of exporting to the EU ([Argus](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2766194-viewpoint-risks-mount-for-indian-steel-exports-in-2026)) |
| Net debt / EBITDA; capex plans | Expansions are ₹5,000–8,000 Cr per MT; leverage at the peak funds capacity that arrives at the trough |
| Green steel / carbon intensity | Policy and CBAM exposure |

**Valuation**: EV/tonne vs replacement cost; EV/EBITDA on mid-cycle EBITDA per tonne; P/B at troughs. **Red flags**:
capex ramp funded by peak-cycle debt; inventory build ahead of price cuts; safeguard-duty dependence. **Companies**:
Tata Steel, JSW Steel, SAIL, Jindal Steel & Power, Jindal Stainless, APL Apollo (pipes).

## 2. Non-ferrous metals & mining

Aluminium (Hindalco, NALCO, Vedanta), zinc/lead/silver (Hindustan Zinc), copper (Hindalco, Vedanta), iron ore
(NMDC), coal (Coal India). Price is LME/global; costs are power (aluminium is "solid electricity" — captive coal
power is the moat), bauxite/ore grades, and for miners, reserve quality and government royalty/pricing.

| KPI | Notes |
|:--|:--|
| LME price and premiums; realisation | Dollar price × INR |
| Cost of production per tonne; cost-curve quartile | Hindustan Zinc and NALCO sit in the first quartile globally — verify current |
| Volumes; capacity; captive power; captive mines | Hindalco's Novelis (US/EU rolling) is a separate, less cyclical business — SOTP |
| Government pricing (coal, iron ore), royalties, District Mineral Fund | Policy drivers |
| Dividend policy (PSU miners: DIPAM rules) | Cash returns at peaks |

**Valuation**: EV/EBITDA on a price deck with sensitivity per $100/t; P/B; dividend yield as a check, not an
anchor ([07.5](../07-special-valuation/05-holdcos-conglomerates-psus-mncs.md) for PSU considerations).

## 3. Cement

**How it makes money.** Limestone + coal/petcoke + power → clinker → cement, sold regionally (freight makes cement
a ~300-km-radius business) to individual home builders (60–65% of demand), infrastructure and real estate.
Regional supply-demand sets prices; a few players per region can hold price discipline — until capacity arrives.

| KPI | Notes |
|:--|:--|
| Capacity (MT), utilisation, volumes | Industry ~650 MT capacity, utilisation ~70–75%; 160–170 MT of additions over FY26–28, 70–75 MT commissioning in FY26 alone ([CRISIL via S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/fertilizers/111225-indias-cement-capacity-to-add-160-170-mil-mt-in-fy-2026-28-crisil)) — utilisation will fall before demand catches up |
| Realisation per tonne (by region) | The price variable; South and East are structurally weakest |
| EBITDA per tonne | ₹700–1,500/t across the cycle; leaders (Shree, UltraTech) at the top |
| Cost per tonne: power & fuel (petcoke/coal), freight (lead distance), raw material | Fuel is 30–35% of cost; green power and WHRS (waste-heat recovery) lower it |
| Consolidation | Adani (ACC/Ambuja) and UltraTech acquisitions since 2022 concentrated the top two at ~40%+ of capacity — verify; pricing discipline vs volume push is the live question |
| Net debt / EBITDA; capex per tonne | Brownfield ₹700–900 Cr/MT; greenfield ₹1,000–1,200 Cr/MT — verify recent announcements |

**Valuation**: EV/tonne (₹ Cr per MT) against replacement cost and acquisition benchmarks; EV/EBITDA on mid-cycle
EBITDA/tonne × volumes; regional pricing scenarios. **Red flags**: volume growth via price cuts in a
consolidating region; capex per tonne well above peers; related-party limestone/logistics. **Companies**: UltraTech,
Ambuja/ACC, Shree, Dalmia Bharat, JK Cement, Ramco, Nuvoco, JK Lakshmi, Birla Corp.

## 4. Chemicals

Two very different businesses under one sector tag:

| | Commodity chemicals | Specialty chemicals |
|:--|:--|:--|
| What | Caustic soda, chlor-alkali, soda ash, PVC, methanol, phenol, fertilisers (regulated) | Agrochemical intermediates, pharma intermediates, fluorochemicals, performance chemicals, CRAMS |
| Economics | Price-taker; global capacity cycles (China); power-intensive | Product/process know-how; customer qualification (switching costs); China+1 |
| Margin | 10–25%, cyclical | 20–35% at the 2021–22 peak; compressed to 12–20% in 2023–25 as Chinese pricing and the capex overshoot hit |
| KPIs | Realisation, spreads over feedstock, capacity, utilisation, power cost | Volume growth, product pipeline, customer count/concentration, capex vs contracted demand, R&D |
| Cycle 2021–26 | Post-COVID spike, then normalisation | The classic capital cycle: multiples of 50–80x in 2021, a wave of capex announcements, Chinese dumping and destocking in 2023–24, margins and multiples halved; recovery in 2025–26 is gradual and company-specific (verify current) |

**Valuation**: commodity — mid-cycle spreads, EV/EBITDA, P/B; specialty — DCF with explicit capex-to-revenue
conversion, P/E vs growth with the 2023–24 experience as the base rate for how fast "structural" margins can
compress. **Red flags**: capex announced as % of current capacity > 50% across the industry; "specialty" labels on
commodity-like products; customer concentration; environmental compliance issues (Gujarat/Maharashtra plant
closures); related-party trading entities. **Companies**: SRF, PI Industries, Navin Fluorine, Deepak Nitrite,
Aarti Industries, Atul, Vinati, Clean Science, Gujarat Fluorochemicals (specialty/fluoro); Tata Chemicals, GHCL,
DCM Shriram, Chemplast (commodity); Coromandel, Chambal, GSFC (fertilisers — subsidy-driven, [05.2](../05-business-analysis/02-industry-analysis.md)).

## 5. Common accounting quirks

Inventory valuation swings (NRV write-downs in price falls); capitalised borrowing costs during expansions;
captive-power and mining subsidiaries (consolidation, transfer pricing); export incentives; carbon credits;
government royalty and DMF provisions; PSU miners' dividend and OFS dynamics; JV accounting (JSW/JFE, Tata/Thyssen
history); goodwill from overseas acquisitions (Tata–Corus, Hindalco–Novelis) and its impairment history.

## 6. The 10-question sector checklist

1. Where is the commodity price vs its 10-year range and vs the marginal producer's cost?
2. Where does this company sit on the cost curve, and why (captive inputs, scale, technology)?
3. Industry capacity additions over the next three years vs demand growth?
4. Trade policy: duties (safeguard, anti-dumping), export restrictions, CBAM?
5. Normalised EBITDA per tonne (or spread), and current vs normalised?
6. Balance sheet through the trough: net debt/EBITDA at mid-cycle and trough EBITDA?
7. Capex plans: per-tonne cost vs replacement benchmarks; timing vs the cycle?
8. Regional pricing and consolidation (cement); customer qualification and concentration (specialty chemicals)?
9. Regulatory and environmental exposure?
10. EV/tonne, P/B and mid-cycle EV/EBITDA vs history and peers — and what phase does the price assume?
