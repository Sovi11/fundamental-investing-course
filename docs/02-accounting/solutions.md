# Module 02 · Solutions

All Kaveri figures are ₹ Cr from the running-example page; arithmetic verified in Python.

## Warm-up

**E02.01** Assets = Liabilities + Equity. (a) Buying inventory for cash: inventory up, cash down — assets only.
(b) Taking a bank loan: cash up, borrowings up. (c) Issuing shares for cash: cash up, equity up (or earning a
profit: assets up, retained earnings up).

**E02.02** *Accrual*: recognising revenue/expense when earned/incurred, not when cash moves. *Deferred revenue*:
cash received before performance — a liability. *Accrued expense*: cost incurred, not yet paid — a liability.
*Prepaid expense*: cash paid before the benefit — an asset.

**E02.03** Identify the contract; identify performance obligations; determine the transaction price; allocate
the price to obligations; recognise revenue as each obligation is satisfied.

**E02.04** Schedule III lines: revenue from operations, other income, exceptional items, PBT, OCI. Analyst
conventions: EBITDA, EBIT, adjusted EPS.

**E02.05** Home-grown brands and reputation; trained people and processes; contingent liabilities (disclosed,
not recorded); operating capacity/market position; promoter-level risks (pledges); order books; the fair value of
assets held at cost (land).

**E02.06** CFO: taxes paid. CFI: purchase of liquid funds (and interest received). CFF: interest paid on term
loans, dividends paid, lease payments (principal and interest, under Kaveri's policy).

**E02.07** *CWIP*: capital work-in-progress — assets under construction, not yet depreciated. *ROU asset*: the
right to use a leased asset, capitalised under Ind AS 116. *DTL*: tax that will be payable in future because
book profit has been recognised ahead of taxable profit (timing differences). *NCI*: outside shareholders' share of
a subsidiary's equity and profit. *Goodwill*: purchase price above the fair value of identifiable net assets
acquired.

**E02.08** LIFO charges the most recent (highest, in inflation) costs to COGS, reducing profit and leaving old
cheap costs in inventory; Ind AS/IFRS prohibit it as not representing physical flows. A US LIFO company will
show lower gross margin and lower inventory than an Indian FIFO/weighted-average company with identical
economics in rising-price periods; adjust with the LIFO reserve.

**E02.18** Tax depreciation (Income-tax Act rates, often WDV) exceeds book depreciation (Schedule II lives) in

the future liability. The ₹1.0 Cr charge is part of the P&L tax expense but is not paid, so it is excluded from
taxes paid in CFO (Kaveri's CFS deducts only current tax of ₹29.5 Cr).

**E02.23** Three performance obligations: pump (point in time — on delivery/control transfer, March), installation

billed but not performed. Booking all ₹5 lakh in March would be premature recognition

## Stretch

**E02.27** Revenue jumps in the change year (contracts in progress are recognised on progress rather than
completion); unbilled revenue/contract assets appear on the balance sheet; CFO is unaffected by the policy (cash
collections unchanged) so CFO/PAT falls. Ind AS 8 requires disclosure of the nature and reason for the change and
its effect on each line, with retrospective restatement of comparatives where practicable.

**E02.28** Year 1: capitaliser shows EBITDA +30, EBIT +24 (amortisation 6), PAT +18 (at 25%), CFO +30 (or +22.5 net
of tax), CFI −30, FCF (CFO − capex) *identical* to the expenser's. Steady state (year 6+): amortisation = 30 = the
expenser's cost, so EBIT and PAT converge; EBITDA stays 30 higher; CFO stays 30 higher and CFI 30 lower; FCF equal.
The difference is presentation and timing; FCF is the invariant.

**E02.29** The cash sits in subsidiaries (possibly overseas, possibly with their own lenders or regulatory
constraints) while the parent — the entity that pays dividends from *its* distributable profits and cash — has
little. Standalone statements would show low parent cash, investments in and loans to subsidiaries, and perhaps
dividend income only when subsidiaries upstream it.

**E02.30** P&L: inside cost of materials consumed. Balance sheet: any unpaid amount in trade payables (disclosed
separately as related-party payables). CFS: within operating cash flows. Ind AS 24 requires the nature of the
relationship, transaction types and amounts, outstanding balances and terms, and provisions for doubtful debts;
"arm's length" is the company's assertion that terms equal those available with unrelated parties — approved by
the audit committee, not independently verified in the note.

**E02.31** Choices: ECL allowance on receivables (₹4.0 Cr vs ~₹15–18 Cr at 25–30% coverage of overdues: PAT −₹8 to
−₹10 Cr); useful lives (Schedule II — a shift to shorter lives might add ₹5–8 Cr of depreciation: PAT −₹4 to −₹6 Cr);
exceptional-item classification (none in FY26); revenue timing on solar systems (unknown effect without the
contract-balances note; potentially material); SBC valuation (₹2.4 Cr, small); deferred tax (small). Conservative
end: PAT roughly ₹75–80 Cr vs ₹90.5 Cr reported — a 12–17% haircut, mostly provisioning.
