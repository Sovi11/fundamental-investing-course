## Warm-up

**E04.01** FY21–26: (1,318/612)^(1/5) − 1 = **16.6%**; FY23–26: (1,318/874)^(1/3) − 1 = **14.7%**. The longer
window includes FY22's +24% (a low COVID base in FY21); the three-year window is the better guide to the current
trajectory.

**E04.02** Gross margin moves → price, input cost, mix. EBITDA margin (gross stable) → overheads/scale. EBIT margin
(EBITDA stable) → depreciation (capex). PAT margin (EBIT stable) → interest, other income, exceptional items, tax.

**E04.03** ROE = PAT ÷ average equity (post-tax). ROCE = (EBIT + other income) ÷ average (equity + borrowings +
lease liabilities) — **pre-tax**. ROIC = EBIT × (1 − t) ÷ average invested capital (net fixed assets + CWIP + ROU +
intangibles + NWC), post-tax, excluding cash.

**E04.04** DIO = inventory/COGS × 365; DSO = receivables/revenue × 365; DPO = payables/COGS × 365; CCC = DIO + DSO −
DPO (days of operating cash tied up between paying suppliers and collecting from customers).

**E04.05** Net debt = borrowings (+ leases) − cash − liquid investments. Net debt/EBITDA = years of EBITDA to repay.
Interest cover = EBIT ÷ finance costs. DSCR = (EBITDA − cash taxes) ÷ (interest + scheduled principal).

**E04.06** Basic EPS = PAT to owners ÷ weighted shares; diluted adds in-the-money options/convertibles
(treasury-stock method); BVPS = equity ÷ shares; payout = dividends ÷ PAT; FCF yield = FCF ÷ market cap.

**E04.07** (PAT − CFO) ÷ average total assets: the non-cash share of profit. Sloan (1996) showed high-accrual
firms' earnings are less persistent and their subsequent stock returns lower, because investors under-discount
accruals.

## Stretch

**E04.27** Average capital ≈ closing/1.15 (halfway between opening = closing/1.3 and closing); ROCE on average ≈
22% × 1.15 ≈ **25%**. Closing-capital ROCE understates returns for fast growers (capital arrived late in the year
and has not yet earned); the reverse bias applies when capital shrinks.

**E04.28** ROE = ROA + (ROA − 6) × D/E. A: 18 = ROA + (ROA − 6) × 0.1 → 1.1 ROA = 18.6 → ROA **16.9%**. B: 18 = ROA +
(ROA − 6) × 2 → 3 ROA = 30 → ROA **10%**. ROA −4 pp: A → 12.9 + 6.9 × 0.1 = **13.6%**; B → 6 + 0 × 2 = **6.0%**. B's
ROE is a leverage artefact and collapses when ROA falls.

**E04.29** Model: "Kaveri's ROE moved with its net margin alone; asset turnover has been ~1.2 since FY22 and
leverage ~1.6, so the company is not becoming more or less capital-efficient. Research time belongs on the margin
drivers — solar mix, tender pricing, copper pass-through, Hosur utilisation — and on the receivables that make the
margin's cash value doubtful, not on the plant or the balance sheet."

**E04.30** A company whose sales grow 25% a year with margins expanding 1 pp a year but with receivable days rising
20 days a year and capex at 8% of sales: EBITDA margin rises; ΔNWC and capex grow faster than EBITDA; CFO/EBITDA
falls and FCF turns more negative each year. The mechanism: profit is recognised on sales that are financed by the
company and by its shareholders.
