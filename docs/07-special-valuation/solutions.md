# Module 07 · Solutions

## Warm-up

**E07.01** Interest expense is the cost of goods, deposits/borrowings are operating liabilities, loan growth is
the business's investing flow, and leverage is regulated — so EBITDA, FCFF, EV and WACC have no meaning.
Replacements: NII (revenue), PPOP (EBITDA), earnings and book value (FCF), equity value and P/B (EV), cost of
equity (WACC).

**E07.02** NIM: NII ÷ average interest-earning assets (or loans). Cost-to-income: opex ÷ (NII + fees). Credit
cost: provisions ÷ average loans. GNPA/NNPA: gross / net (of stage-3 provisions) non-performing loans ÷ loans. PCR:
stage-3 provisions ÷ stage-3 loans. Slippages: loans newly turning NPA. CRAR: (Tier-1 + Tier-2) ÷ RWA. ALM:
maturity matching of assets and liabilities. Stages 1/2/3: performing / 30+ dpd (SICR) / 90+ dpd credit-impaired.

**E07.03** EV: net worth + PV of in-force profits. VNB: PV of profits on the year's new policies. VNB margin: VNB ÷
APE. Persistency: share of policies still in force at 13/25/37/61 months. Combined ratio: (claims + expenses) ÷
premium. Float: premiums held before claims are paid. TER: fund expense ratio charged to investors. Take rate:
platform revenue ÷ transaction value.

**E07.04** Cyclicals show low P/Es at earnings peaks and high/meaningless P/Es at troughs; use P/B and EV/tonne
(or EV/replacement cost) at troughs, and P/E on normalised earnings.

**E07.05** Contribution margin: revenue − variable costs per unit. Rule of 40: growth % + margin % ≥ 40. Implied
terminal multiple: EV grown at the discount rate ÷ terminal-year EBITDA. Holdco discount: 1 − price/NAV. NAV: PV of
project cash flows + assets − debt. Regulated equity base: the equity on which a regulator allows a return. ARPU:
revenue per user per month.

**E07.06** Provisioning moves from incurred-loss (IRAC) to expected credit loss (stages 1/2/3) under RBI's 2026
Directions, effective 1-Apr-2027 with a glide path to 31-Mar-2031.

## Stretch

**E07.26** Under clean surplus, dividends = PAT − ΔBook, so the DDM/DCF sum of dividends equals book + PV of (PAT
− $k_e$ × book) after rearrangement. Divergence example: a residual-income terminal that grows RI at 6% while the
DCF terminal grows dividends at 6% with a payout inconsistent with RoE and growth (payout ≠ 1 − g/RoE) — the two
imply different reinvestment and give different values; fix by deriving payout from g/RoE.

**E07.27** For: same currency of returns (RoE), same regulator, same deposit franchise economics; PSU RoEs in
FY24–26 matched private peers. Against: government ownership (policy lending, capital calls, OFS overhang,
management tenure), lower sustainable RoE through the cycle, and a discount for the probability of
non-commercial decisions — a parallel line below the private one, whose gap is the policy-risk premium.

**E07.28** (1) Reported EBITDA (with ESOP cost): −6% of revenue. (2) Pre-Ind AS 116 EBITDA (lease payments back in
opex): lower still by the lease cost. (3) Free cash flow after leases and capex: the honest number. Use (3) for
runway and value, (1) for profitability comparisons, and never the "adjusted" figure.

**E07.29** (a) A tariff hike raises expected asset value and lowers its uncertainty — the call moves deeper into
the money: large gain. (b) Conversion at a discount transfers value to the government and dilutes the option's
per-share claim, but reduces the strike (debt): mixed — per-share value often falls even as survival probability
rises. (c) AGR relief lowers the strike directly: gain, largest of the three per rupee of liability removed.
