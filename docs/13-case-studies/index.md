# Module 13 · Case Studies

**What this module does.** Puts everything from Modules 00–12 to work on 25 real decisions — ten global and fifteen
Indian — each frozen at a date when a thoughtful investor had to act with only the information then public. You read
the scene and the numbers, answer the questions *before* reading what happened, and then compare your reasoning with
the outcome, the model answers and the signals that were knowable at the time.

**Why it matters.** Frameworks are easy to agree with and hard to apply. Cases are where you find out whether you can
spot a fictitious cash balance, price a tax risk, value a lender, survive a cyclical trough or resist a great company
at a bad price — with a real share price in front of you and no hindsight. They are also the fastest way to build
pattern recognition: the same handful of mistakes and signals recur across countries and decades.

## How to use a case

1. **Respect the decision date.** Sections 1–3 contain only what was public at the time. Do not look up the company.
2. **Answer section 3 in writing, with numbers, before reading on.** Time-box it (the "Time" row gives a guide; about
   half of it goes on sections 1–3). Use the course tools (`fi.valuation`, `fi.forensics`) or a spreadsheet.
3. **Write a decision-journal entry** ([11.6](../11-process/06-behavioural-finance-and-decision-journals.md)): buy,
   avoid or short; size; confidence; what would change your mind. Keep it — the casebook template works.
4. **Read section 4 (what happened) and section 5 (knowable vs hindsight)** and score yourself honestly: right for the
   right reasons, right for the wrong reasons, wrong for the right reasons, or wrong.
5. **Only then open the model answers** (section 7). Where your numbers differ, find out why.
6. **Do one discussion question** from section 8; several point to a live company you can analyse today.

!!! tip "Two decision points"
    Many Indian cases have two decision dates (A and B) years apart, often at opposite ends of a cycle or a
    re-rating. Do decision A completely — including your journal entry — before reading decision B's scene.

## Patterns across cases

1. **Cash earns interest; check the yield.** Satyam's ₹5,000 crore and Wirecard's €2 billion of "cash" earned far less
   than the deposits they supposedly were. The yield test takes two minutes. → [I1](india/01-satyam-2009.md) ·
   [G9](global/09-wirecard-2020.md) · [09.4](../09-forensics/04-cash-flow-games.md)
2. **Companies with surplus cash do not borrow — and their promoters do not pledge everything.** Satyam borrowed while
   "cash-rich"; Wirecard kept raising debt and a convertible; Satyam's and several other promoters' pledges were
   margin-called. → [I1](india/01-satyam-2009.md) · [G9](global/09-wirecard-2020.md) · [I9](india/09-adani-hindenburg-2023.md)
3. **Find where the profit comes from.** Enron's mark-to-market gains, Valeant's "cash EPS", Wirecard's third-party
   acquirers, Vakrangee's low-margin revenue surge and Jet's asset-sale "other income" each hid how little the core
   business earned. → [G4](global/04-enron-2001.md) · [G8](global/08-valeant-2015.md) · [G9](global/09-wirecard-2020.md) ·
   [I11](india/11-jet-kingfisher-airlines.md) · [I14](india/14-manpasand-vakrangee-small-cap-flags.md)
4. **Liquidity kills before solvency.** Lehman, IL&FS and DHFL, Jet Airways and Tata Motors in 2009 all faced the same
   question — can the debt due in the next twelve months be rolled? The asset side decided who deserved to survive;
   the liability side decided who did. → [G6](global/06-lehman-2008.md) · [I4](india/04-ilfs-dhfl-2018.md) ·
   [I11](india/11-jet-kingfisher-airlines.md) · [I15](india/15-tata-motors-jlr.md) · [I3](india/03-bajaj-finance-2008-2019.md)
5. **A great company is not a great investment at any price.** Cisco at 230× earnings, Coca-Cola in 1998, Asian Paints
   at 97× and DMart at its peak all delivered business growth and poor or negative returns for years.
   → [G3](global/03-cisco-2000.md) · [G2](global/02-coca-cola-1988.md) · [I2](india/02-asian-paints-compounder.md) ·
   [I13](india/13-dmart-avenue-supermarts.md)
6. **Decompose every past return into earnings growth and re-rating.** Asian Paints' 25% a year (2011–22), Bajaj
   Finance's 62% (2010–19) and ITC's 47% (2020–23) came roughly half to two-thirds from re-rating — the part that
   cannot repeat and often reverses.
   → [I2](india/02-asian-paints-compounder.md) · [I3](india/03-bajaj-finance-2008-2019.md) · [I12](india/12-itc-value-trap-or-not.md) ·
   [G10](global/10-apple-2016.md)
7. **High returns attract capital; the capital cycle eventually arrives.** Jio in telecom, Birla Opus in paints,
   IndiGo in aviation, Juniper against Cisco, the iPhone against Nokia and BlackBerry. The announcement usually comes
   years before the damage — and the stock often ignores it. → [I10](india/10-vodafone-idea-telecom-war.md) ·
   [I2](india/02-asian-paints-compounder.md) · [I11](india/11-jet-kingfisher-airlines.md) · [G7](global/07-nokia-blackberry-2007-2013.md)
8. **Gatekeepers certify what they test.** Big Four auditors (Satyam, Wirecard, Vakrangee, Manpasand), rating agencies
   (IL&FS, Enron), regulators (BaFin) and governance awards all failed to catch problems outside their procedures.
   An auditor's resignation is the most important governance signal in Indian small caps.
   → [I1](india/01-satyam-2009.md) · [G9](global/09-wirecard-2020.md) · [I4](india/04-ilfs-dhfl-2018.md) ·
   [I14](india/14-manpasand-vakrangee-small-cap-flags.md)
9. **At the trough, value the troubled piece by subtraction.** Tata Motors in 2009 priced JLR's equity near zero;
   Nokia's patents and net cash set a floor; ITC's cigarette business was priced at 6.5× EBITDA once the rest was
   valued. → [I15](india/15-tata-motors-jlr.md) · [G7](global/07-nokia-blackberry-2007-2013.md) · [I12](india/12-itc-value-trap-or-not.md)
10. **Lenders are RoA × leverage, and the equity owns the variance of credit cost.** Bajaj Finance, HDFC Bank, Yes Bank
    and DHFL differ less in their stated strategy than in how their credit cost and funding behaved under stress.
    → [I3](india/03-bajaj-finance-2008-2019.md) · [I7](india/07-hdfc-bank-consistency.md) · [I5](india/05-yes-bank-2018-2020.md) ·
    [I4](india/04-ilfs-dhfl-2018.md)
11. **Allegations are not findings — in either direction.** Hindenburg's allegations against Adani, the claims against
    Vakrangee's trading and the promoters of Jet and Kingfisher each led to different official outcomes. Precision about
    what was found protects you from over- and under-reacting. → [I9](india/09-adani-hindenburg-2023.md) ·
    [I14](india/14-manpasand-vakrangee-small-cap-flags.md) · [I11](india/11-jet-kingfisher-airlines.md)
12. **Size for the tail you cannot hedge.** Governance jumps, tax changes, regulatory orders and operational shocks (a
    cyberattack at JLR, the RBI's embargo on Bajaj Finance, the excise on ITC) sit outside most models. The answer is
    position size, not prediction. → [11.4](../11-process/04-position-sizing-and-portfolio-construction.md)
