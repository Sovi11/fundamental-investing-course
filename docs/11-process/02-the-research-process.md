# 11.2 · The research process

> **Why this matters:** research without a process either never finishes (there is always another note to read)
> or finishes too early (the story was convincing). A stage-gated process fixes both: each stage has a time
> budget, a deliverable and a question that decides whether the next stage is worth the time. This lesson gives
> you the three stages — kill test, triage, deep dive — the checklist that runs through them, and the research
> log that makes the work cumulative.

**Learning objectives** — after this lesson you can:

- Run a 30-minute kill test, a 3-hour triage and a 2–3-week deep dive, each with defined outputs and gate
  questions.
- Prioritise primary sources and sequence the reading.
- Keep a research log that supports later review and compliance.
- Manage time, avoid research-as-procrastination, and know when to stop.

**Prerequisites:** [11.1 Idea generation](01-idea-generation.md), [03.2 Anatomy of an annual report](../03-reading-filings/02-anatomy-of-an-annual-report.md)  ·  **Time:** ~60 min

## 1. Stage gates

| Stage | Budget | Inputs | Deliverable | Gate question |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **0. Queue** | 5 min | The idea and its source | One line in the queue | Is there a plausible reason for mispricing, inside (or near) my circle? |
| **1. Kill test** | 30 min | Screener page; `tools/fi` dashboard; shareholding pattern; last annual report's auditor's report | A one-page kill-test sheet (business in three sentences; 5-year numbers; people; forensic quick scores; valuation snapshot; mispricing reason) | Any disqualifier? If not, what is the one question triage must answer? |
| **2. Triage** | 3 hours | Latest annual report (MD&A, auditor's report, RPT note, contingent liabilities, borrowings, receivables ageing); last four concall transcripts; latest rating rationale; two competitors' latest calls | Triage note: unit economics and KPIs; industry position; say-do table; first reverse DCF; a one-sentence variant perception with the evidence that would refute it | Can I state a variant perception, and is the payoff asymmetric enough to justify three weeks? |
| **3. Deep dive** | 2–3 weeks | Five annual reports; all concalls; DRHP if recent; industry data; scuttlebutt; the model | Full case file; three-statement model with scenarios; forensic checklist; governance checklist; scuttlebutt log; valuation triangulated; catalysts; pre-mortem; thesis-breakers | Do the numbers, the people and the price all pass? What specifically breaks the thesis, and by when would I know? |
| **4. Memo & decision** | 1 day | The case file | One-page thesis; sizing; journal entry | Would I hold this at 2× the size for three years without new information? If not, why is it a position at all? |

The **disqualifiers** at the kill test (from [09.7](../09-forensics/07-the-forensic-checklist.md) and
[05.6](../05-business-analysis/06-corporate-governance-india.md)): a Beneish flag without explanation; CFO/PAT under
60% for three years (non-lender); pledge over 20%; unexplained auditor resignation; substantive SEBI action
against promoters; a business you cannot explain in three sentences; no plausible mispricing reason. Most ideas
die here in twenty minutes, and that is the process working.

## 2. Sequencing the reading

Primary sources first, in this order — because each step tells you what to look for in the next:

1. **Annual report, back to front**: auditor's report (opinion, KAMs, CARO), then the notes that matter (RPTs,
   contingent liabilities, borrowings, receivables ageing, revenue policy), then the financial statements, then
   MD&A, then the chairman's letter *last* (so the narrative does not frame the numbers).
2. **Concall transcripts, oldest to newest** for the last two years: build the say-do table as you go
   ([05.5](../05-business-analysis/05-management-and-capital-allocation.md)); note what analysts asked and what
   was dodged.
3. **Rating rationale**: liquidity, covenants, key sensitivities ([04.5](../04-financial-analysis/05-leverage-solvency-liquidity.md)).
4. **DRHP** if listed within five years: KPIs, risk factors, related parties, pre-IPO history.
5. **Competitors' concalls and reports**: the same market described by people with different incentives.
6. **Industry data** ([05.7](../05-business-analysis/07-scuttlebutt-and-primary-research.md)): the regulator's
   statistics, trade bodies, tender portals.
7. **Sell-side reports** *last*: to learn the consensus and its arguments, once your own view exists
   ([03.5](../03-reading-filings/05-other-documents.md)).
8. **Scuttlebutt**: designed after the reading, because now you know which claims need testing.

## 3. The research checklist

A single page that travels with every case (the casebook's `01-full-case-study.md` is built on it):

| Section | Questions | Modules |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Business | What does it sell, to whom, why; unit economics; value chain; concentration | 05.1 |
| Industry | Structure, forces, life cycle, capital cycle, regulation, substitutes | 05.2, 05.4 |
| Moat | Source; evidence in 10 years of ROIC, share, pricing; erosion risks | 05.3 |
| Numbers | Growth, margins, returns, working capital, leverage, quality; 10-year dashboard | 04.1–04.7 |
| Filings | Auditor's report, notes, policy changes, say-do table | 03.2–03.4 |
| Forensics | 40-point checklist; scores; RAG and verdict | 09.7 |
| Governance | Promoter, RPTs, pledges, board, auditor, issuances, regulators | 05.6, 09.5 |
| Scuttlebutt | Plan, conversations, data checks, log | 05.7 |
| Valuation | DCF (scenarios, sensitivities), multiples vs peers/history, reverse DCF, expected value | 06.3–06.8, 07.x |
| Thesis | Variant perception; catalysts; risks; pre-mortem; thesis-breakers with dates | 11.3 |
| Decision | Size; entry plan; monitoring KPIs; journal | 11.4–11.6 |

## 4. The research log

A dated, append-only record of what you read, whom you spoke to, what you learned, what surprised you and what
you expected — one file per company in the casebook. It serves three purposes: it makes the next quarter's update
an increment rather than a restart; it lets you audit your own process after the outcome (was the thesis right for
the right reasons?); and it is your compliance record — what you knew and when, and that nothing you learned was
UPSI ([05.7 §5](../05-business-analysis/07-scuttlebutt-and-primary-research.md)).

Minimum entry: date · source (document/person type) · claim or fact · confidence · what it changes · next action.

## 5. Time management

- **Timebox each stage** and stop when the budget ends; extend only with a written reason.
- **Kill fast.** The cost of a wrong kill is a missed idea (there are thousands); the cost of a wrong keep is three
  weeks and, worse, an anchored view.
- **Research as procrastination**: the tell is reading a fifteenth document without a changed conclusion. If the
  last three sources changed nothing, you are done — write the memo.
- **Parallelise cheaply**: run kill tests on five ideas in an evening; run one deep dive at a time.
- **Diminishing returns are steep**: the first annual report and four concalls give 80% of the information; the
  fifth annual report gives 5%. Spend the marginal hour on scuttlebutt or the model, not on the tenth note.
- **Calendar the process**: results seasons (Apr–May, Jul–Aug, Oct–Nov, Jan–Feb) are for updates; the quiet
  months are for deep dives.

## 6. When to stop

Stop researching and decide when: the variant perception is stated with evidence and a refutation test; the
valuation is triangulated and the price sits clearly on one side of the distribution (or clearly does not); the
forensic and governance checklists are scored; the thesis-breakers are written with dates; and the last three
sources changed nothing. Stop *and kill* when: a disqualifier appears at any stage; the variant perception cannot
be stated; the payoff is symmetric; or the time budget has doubled without convergence.

!!! tip "Trader's lens"
    A stage-gated process is a stop-loss on research time. Traders know that the hardest discipline is cutting a
    position that has consumed effort; analysts face the same sunk-cost pull with a company they have spent three
    weeks on. The gate questions are pre-committed exits — decide the criteria before the work, and honour them.

!!! info "India notes"
    - Exchange announcement feeds (BSE/NSE corporate announcements, filterable by company) are the research log's
      raw timeline; download them at the start of a deep dive.
    - Concall transcripts are mandatory disclosures (LODR, 2023 amendments) and sit on the exchange sites and
      company IR pages; older ones are on aggregator sites.
    - The results calendar is public (companies announce board-meeting dates); build the monitoring schedule from it.

!!! warning "Common mistakes"
    - Starting with the chairman's letter and the sell-side report.
    - Skipping the kill test because the idea came from someone credible.
    - Deep-diving three ideas at once and finishing none.
    - No research log — the same document read three times across two years.
    - Confusing "I have read everything" with "I have a view".

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|:--|:--|
| **Stage gate** | A decision point with a predefined question that must be answered before more time is spent |
| **Kill test** | A 30-minute screen for disqualifiers and a mispricing reason |
| **Triage** | A 3-hour read to state a variant perception and payoff asymmetry |
| **Deep dive** | The full multi-week research producing the case file and model |
| **Say-do table** | Record of management guidance vs delivery |
| **Research log** | Dated record of sources, findings, confidence and next actions |
| **Timebox** | A fixed time budget for a task |
| **Research-as-procrastination** | Continuing to gather information after conclusions have stopped changing |

## Check your understanding

1. An idea passes the kill test with a "yes" on every disqualifier check but you cannot write the one question
   triage must answer. Proceed?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>No. Without a specific question there is no hypothesis, and triage becomes
open-ended reading. Return it to the queue with "no mispricing hypothesis" and move on.</details>

2. Why read the auditor's report before the MD&A?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The auditor's report and notes are the least narrative-laden parts of the report;
reading them first means the MD&A's story is tested against facts you already hold rather than framing how you
read the facts.</details>

3. Three weeks into a deep dive, the valuation is ambiguous (price near the probability-weighted value) and the
   thesis-breakers are clear. What is the output?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A watchlist entry with a target entry price, the monitoring KPIs and the
thesis-breakers — a complete, valuable output. The research was not wasted; the decision is "not at this price".</details>

4. Which four documents give most of the information for a triage, and in what order?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>The latest annual report (auditor's report and key notes first), the last four
concall transcripts (oldest first), the latest rating rationale, and two competitors' latest concalls.</details>
