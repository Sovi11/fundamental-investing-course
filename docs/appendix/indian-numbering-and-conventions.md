# Indian numbering, dates & conventions

Indian filings, broker reports and websites use conventions that trip up newcomers. Keep this page open during the
first few weeks.

## 1. Lakh and crore

| Indian unit | Digits | International equivalent | Written as |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 thousand | 1,000 | 1 thousand | ₹1,000 |
| 1 lakh | 1,00,000 | 100 thousand | ₹1 lakh / ₹1 L |
| 10 lakh | 10,00,000 | 1 million | ₹10 lakh |
| 1 crore | 1,00,00,000 | 10 million | ₹1 Cr |
| 100 crore | 1,00,00,00,000 | 1 billion | ₹100 Cr |
| 1 lakh crore | 1,00,000 crore | 1 trillion | ₹1 lakh Cr / ₹1 L Cr |

Indian digit grouping puts commas after the first three digits from the right and then every two digits:
`12,34,56,789` = 12 crore 34 lakh 56 thousand 789. This course writes crore amounts with international grouping
(`₹1,318 Cr`) because that is what most annual reports and data sites do.

**Quick conversions** (useful for mental maths):

- ₹1 Cr ≈ US$ 0.11–0.12 mn at ₹85–90/US$ (check the current rate). ₹1,000 Cr ≈ US$ 110–120 mn.
- Market-cap language: "a ₹10,000 Cr company" ≈ US$ 1.1–1.2 bn.
- Per-share figures are in plain rupees (EPS ₹15.1), while company totals are in crore — never mix them in one
  formula without converting (market cap in ₹ Cr = price in ₹ × shares in crore).

!!! warning "Units in filings"
    Check the top of every statement: "₹ in crore", "₹ in lakh", "₹ in million" and "₹ in thousands" are all used
    by Indian companies. Screener.in shows ₹ Cr. Many IT companies and some MNC subsidiaries report in ₹ million or
    ₹ lakh. A 10× or 100× unit error is the most common beginner mistake.

## 2. Financial years and quarters

Indian companies (with rare exceptions) use an **April–March** financial year.

| Label | Period | Results usually announced |
|:--|:--|:--|
| FY26 (also "FY2025-26", "2025-26") | 1-Apr-2025 → 31-Mar-2026 | Q4 & full year: April–May 2026 (audited annual results due within 60 days of year-end) |
| Q1 FY27 | Apr–Jun 2026 | July–mid-August 2026 (within 45 days of quarter-end) |
| Q2 FY27 / H1 FY27 | Jul–Sep 2026 / Apr–Sep 2026 | October–mid-November |
| Q3 FY27 / 9M FY27 | Oct–Dec 2026 / Apr–Dec 2026 | January–mid-February |
| Q4 FY27 | Jan–Mar 2027 | April–May 2027 |

- "**FY27E**" / "FY27F" = estimate/forecast for FY27. "**TTM**" / "LTM" = trailing twelve months (sum of the last four
  quarters). "**YoY**" = vs the same quarter last year; "**QoQ**" (or "sequential") = vs the previous quarter.
- The **assessment year** (AY) in tax is the year *after* the financial year: income of FY26 is assessed in AY 2026-27
  (terminology under the Income-tax Act, 1961; the Income-tax Act, 2025 moves to a single "tax year" concept — check the
  current terminology when reading tax documents).
- US companies use calendar years or their own fiscal years (Apple's FY ends in late September). Always check.

## 4. Numbers in tables

- Negative numbers appear as `(14.0)` in financial statements, `-14.0` elsewhere.
- "Cr" is often written as "cr", "Crs" or "Crore". "Mn" = million; "Bn" = billion; "L" = lakh.
- Percent vs percentage points: a margin moving from 12% to 14% is **+200 bps** or **+2 percentage points** — not +2%.
- Share prices on Indian sites are **not** adjusted for all corporate actions unless stated — check splits and bonus
  issues before computing long-period returns.
