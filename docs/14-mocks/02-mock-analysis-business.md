# Mock 2 — Analysis & business quality

**Take after:** Modules 04 and 05. **Time:** 2 hours, closed book (calculator allowed). **Marks:** 80 — Part A 25,
Part B 30, Part C 25. **Pass:** 56 (70%).

**Rules:** show workings in Parts B and C. Amounts in ₹ crore unless stated. Use the
[Kaveri running example](../appendix/running-example/kaveri-pumps.md) figures given in the questions; do not look them
up. Mark with the [answer key](answer-keys/02-mock-analysis-business-key.md).

## Part A — multiple choice (25 × 1 mark; ~30 minutes)

**A1.** Revenue grows 15%, EBITDA margin is flat, and depreciation grows 40% after a new plant. EBIT grows:
(a) faster than revenue · (b) at 15% · (c) slower than revenue · (d) cannot be less than EBITDA growth

**A2.** A company's gross margin falls from 36.8% to 34.9% while its EBITDA margin falls from 15.4% to 13.8%. The most
likely main cause is:
(a) higher employee costs · (b) higher raw-material costs or a lower-margin mix · (c) higher depreciation · (d) higher
interest

**A3.** ROCE differs from ROE mainly because:
(a) ROCE uses PAT · (b) ROCE measures returns on all capital (debt and equity) before financing · (c) ROE ignores
leverage · (d) ROCE includes goodwill only

**A4.** In the three-factor DuPont identity, ROE equals:
(a) net margin × asset turnover × equity multiplier · (b) EBIT margin × asset turnover · (c) net margin ÷ leverage ·
(d) asset turnover × interest cover

**A5.** A company with a 30% ROIC that can reinvest 60% of its NOPAT grows NOPAT at about:
(a) 6% · (b) 18% · (c) 30% · (d) 50%

**A6.** Negative working capital means:
(a) the company is insolvent · (b) customers or suppliers are financing part of the operations · (c) inventory is
negative · (d) the company has more debt than equity

**A7.** An increase in receivable days from 70 to 96 on revenue of ₹1,318 Cr ties up roughly:
(a) ₹25 Cr · (b) ₹94 Cr · (c) ₹150 Cr · (d) ₹350 Cr

**A8.** Interest cover is usually defined as:
(a) EBIT ÷ finance costs · (b) PAT ÷ interest · (c) debt ÷ EBITDA · (d) CFO ÷ debt

**A9.** Net debt ÷ EBITDA of 0.8× for an industrial company is generally:
(a) alarming · (b) conservative · (c) impossible · (d) irrelevant

**A10.** CFO persistently below PAT is most worrying when:
(a) the company is in a capex cycle · (b) receivables are growing faster than revenue · (c) depreciation is high ·
(d) the company pays dividends

**A11.** Diluted EPS growth is lower than net-profit growth when:
(a) the company buys back shares · (b) the share count rises (options, conversions, equity raises) · (c) the tax rate
falls · (d) the company pays dividends

**A12.** A business with high fixed costs and low variable costs has:
(a) low operating leverage · (b) high operating leverage · (c) no pricing power · (d) negative gross margins

**A13.** Which is the clearest evidence of pricing power?
(a) rising market share · (b) raising prices above cost inflation without losing volume · (c) high advertising spend ·
(d) a well-known founder

**A14.** Porter's "threat of substitutes" refers to:
(a) new competitors entering the same industry · (b) products from other industries that meet the same need · (c)
suppliers integrating forward · (d) customers switching brands

**A15.** The capital cycle predicts that high industry returns tend to:
(a) persist indefinitely · (b) attract capacity, which later depresses returns · (c) fall because of regulation only ·
(d) rise as more capital enters

**A16.** Switching costs are a moat when:
(a) customers find it expensive or risky to change supplier · (b) the company has high fixed costs · (c) the product
is a commodity · (d) the founder owns most of the shares

**A17.** Network effects mean:
(a) the product becomes more valuable as more people use it · (b) the company has many branches · (c) distribution is
outsourced · (d) marketing spend is high

**A18.** Which is a governance red flag in an Indian company?
(a) an independent audit committee · (b) a growing share of purchases from a promoter-owned supplier · (c) a
dividend policy · (d) a Big Four auditor

**A19.** A promoter pledge is a risk to minority shareholders mainly because:
(a) it increases dividends · (b) a price fall can trigger forced selling and a change of control · (c) it reduces the
share count · (d) it is illegal

**A20.** "Say-do" analysis compares:
(a) the sell-side's estimates with actuals · (b) management's past guidance and promises with what was delivered ·
(c) the company's price with its value · (d) the board's size with its independence

**A21.** Scuttlebutt research means:
(a) reading only the annual report · (b) gathering information from customers, suppliers, competitors and ex-employees ·
(c) following social media · (d) screening on ratios

**A22.** A company earning a 12% ROIC while reinvesting heavily, with a 12% cost of capital:
(a) creates value by growing · (b) neither creates nor destroys value by growing · (c) destroys value by growing ·
(d) must raise debt

**A23.** Unit economics for a subscription business most directly compare:
(a) revenue and market cap · (b) customer lifetime value and customer acquisition cost · (c) EBITDA and capex ·
(d) price and volume

**A24.** Which statement about margins across industries is correct?
(a) high margins always mean a better business · (b) a low-margin, high-turnover business can earn a high ROCE ·
(c) retailers have higher margins than software companies · (d) margins do not affect ROCE

**A25.** Management's capital allocation is best judged by:
(a) the size of the capex budget · (b) the returns earned on capital retained and deployed over time · (c) the number of
acquisitions · (d) the CEO's statements about priorities

## Part B — ratio problems (30 marks; ~45 minutes)

**B1. DuPont (6 marks).** Kaveri FY26: PAT ₹90.5 Cr; revenue ₹1,318 Cr; total assets ₹1,035.8 Cr (FY25) and ₹1,143.6 Cr
(FY26); equity ₹637.2 Cr (FY25) and ₹706.1 Cr (FY26); PBT ₹121.0 Cr; EBIT ₹134.1 Cr plus other income ₹3.9 Cr. Using
average balances, compute the three-factor DuPont (net margin, asset turnover, equity multiplier) and ROE; then the
tax burden, interest burden and EBIT margin (on EBIT including other income) of the five-factor version.

**B2. Incremental returns (8 marks).** Kaveri's NOPAT (EBIT × (1 − 25.17%)) was ₹91.37 Cr in FY24 and ₹100.35 Cr in
FY26. Invested capital (net fixed assets, CWIP, right-of-use and intangibles plus net working capital) was ₹690.6 Cr
at the end of FY24 and ₹886.8 Cr at the end of FY26; net working capital was ₹193.7 Cr and ₹365.0 Cr. (a) Compute the
incremental ROIC over FY24–FY26. (b) What share of the increase in invested capital was working capital? (c) Compare
with Kaveri's FY26 ROIC of 12.0% and interpret.

**B3. Price, volume and mix (6 marks).** A company sells two products. Year 1: A 100 units at ₹10, B 50 units at ₹20.
Year 2: A 90 units at ₹11, B 70 units at ₹19. Compute revenue growth and split the change into volume (at year-1 average
price), mix (at year-1 prices) and price effects.

**B4. Operating leverage (4 marks).** Revenue ₹500 Cr, variable costs 55% of revenue, fixed costs ₹150 Cr. Compute EBIT
now and after a 10% revenue rise, and the degree of operating leverage. What happens to EBIT if revenue instead falls
10%?

**B5. Earnings quality (6 marks).** Kaveri FY26: PAT ₹90.5 Cr, CFO ₹65.1 Cr, capex ₹48.0 Cr, intangibles purchased ₹4.0
Cr, average total assets ₹1,089.7 Cr, net debt ₹140.5 Cr, EBITDA ₹181.9 Cr, EBIT ₹134.1 Cr, finance costs ₹17.0 Cr.
Compute the balance-sheet accruals ratio ((PAT − CFO) ÷ average total assets), free cash flow and FCF ÷ PAT, net debt ÷
EBITDA and interest cover. Which one number worries you most, and why?

## Part C — business-quality mini-case (25 marks; ~45 minutes)

**Sahyadri Specialty Chemicals Ltd** (fictional) makes intermediates for agrochemical and pharmaceutical companies
under multi-year contracts, from two plants in Maharashtra. It listed in FY20.

| ₹ Cr | FY22 | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Revenue | 820 | 1,010 | 1,190 | 1,260 | 1,410 |
| EBITDA margin | 24% | 25% | 23% | 19% | 18% |
| Capex | 180 | 260 | 320 | 280 | 150 |
| Cash from operations | 150 | 170 | 160 | 190 | 240 |
| Net debt | 120 | 290 | 520 | 640 | 610 |
| ROCE | 26% | 24% | 18% | 13% | 12% |

Other facts: the top three customers are 58% of revenue (the largest, a global agrochemical company, 31%); the plant
commissioned in FY24 runs at 45% utilisation; management attributes the margin decline to "Chinese price competition
and customer destocking" and guides to "margins recovering to 22–24% as utilisation improves"; the promoter family owns
62% with no pledge; the second plant's land is leased from a promoter-group company; the company has filed 14 process
patents; two contracts are "take-or-pay" for three years.

**Required:**

1. (5 marks) Compute EBITDA for each year, free cash flow (CFO − capex) for each year and in total, CFO ÷ EBITDA each
   year, and net debt ÷ EBITDA each year. Compare the revenue and EBITDA CAGRs for FY22–FY26.
2. (5 marks) Compute the increase in EBITDA over FY22–FY26 per rupee of cumulative capex. What does it say about the
   returns on the expansion?
3. (6 marks) Assess the moat: list the sources of competitive advantage the facts support, and the threats (customer
   concentration, China, the capital cycle). Use [05.3](../05-business-analysis/03-moats-and-competitive-advantage.md)
   and [05.4](../05-business-analysis/04-capital-cycle-and-competition.md).
4. (5 marks) Evaluate management's capital allocation and governance, and write two questions for the next earnings
   call that would test the "margins recovering" guidance.
5. (4 marks) Give an overall business-quality verdict (High / Medium / Low) with the three facts that decide it.
