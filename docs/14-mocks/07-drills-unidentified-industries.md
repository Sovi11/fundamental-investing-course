# Drill — "Unidentified industries" (India edition)

> **Why this drill:** a company's economics leave fingerprints on its financial statements — margins, capital
> intensity, working capital, leverage — long before you read a word of its annual report. This classic exercise asks
> you to identify twelve real Indian companies from their ratios alone. It trains the reflex that every number in a
> set of accounts should *make sense for that kind of business*, which is the foundation of both analysis and forensics.

**Take after:** Module 04 ([04.2 Margins](../04-financial-analysis/02-margins-and-cost-structure.md),
[04.3 Returns on capital](../04-financial-analysis/03-returns-on-capital.md),
[04.4 Working capital](../04-financial-analysis/04-working-capital-and-cash-conversion.md)). **Time:** 45 minutes.
**Materials:** this page only. No searching.

## The twelve businesses

Each column below is one of these twelve large Indian listed companies. Match each letter to a business.

1. An **IT services exporter** (software development and outsourcing for global clients)
2. A **large private-sector bank**
3. A **home and personal-care FMCG company** (soaps, detergents, foods, beverages)
4. A **value supermarket chain** that owns most of its stores
5. A **passenger-car manufacturer**
6. A **cement manufacturer**
7. An **integrated steel producer** with operations in India and Europe
8. A **power utility**, mostly regulated thermal generation
9. A **pharmaceutical company** (specialty and generic drugs, large US business)
10. A **telecom operator** (mobile, broadband, with African operations)
11. An **airline**
12. A **real-estate developer** with a large rental (office and mall) portfolio held in a joint venture

!!! note "How the ratios are defined"
    Operating margin = operating profit (before depreciation, interest, other income and tax) ÷ revenue. Inventory and
    payable days are measured on cost of materials, so they can look very high for businesses with small material
    costs. Working-capital days = (current assets − current liabilities, excluding cash and debt) ÷ revenue × 365;
    negative means suppliers, customers or advances fund the business. For the bank, "revenue" is interest earned and
    "debt" is deposits plus borrowings. For one company, FY2026 net profit includes a large one-off gain; for another,
    the share of profit from a joint venture sits in other income. Spotting those is part of the drill.

## Your task

1. **Match** each letter to a business (write your answers before opening the key).
2. For each match, write **the two ratios that decided it** and one sentence explaining why those ratios fit that
   business model.
3. For the three you found hardest, write what *additional* ratio would have settled it.
4. **Bonus:** which company has the highest ROCE, and what feature of its business model explains it? Which has the
   lowest, and is that structural or cyclical?

**Scoring:** 1 mark per correct match (12), 1 mark per sound two-ratio justification (12), up to 6 marks for the
bonus and the "hardest three". 30 marks in all; 22+ is strong.

??? tip "Hints (open only if stuck, and deduct 1 mark per hint)"
    - Look first for the extremes: the lowest asset turnover, the highest D&A, the highest debt/equity, the lowest
      operating margin with the highest asset turnover.
    - Which business collects cash before it delivers the service, and which is paid by customers in advance for
      property it has not yet finished?
    - Which company pays its suppliers in a week?
    - Which business has no inventory, 90+ debtor days and almost no fixed assets?
