## Part B

**B1 (6).** Average assets ₹1,089.7 Cr; average equity ₹671.65 Cr. Net margin 90.5 ÷ 1,318 = **6.87%**; asset turnover
1,318 ÷ 1,089.7 = **1.21×**; equity multiplier 1,089.7 ÷ 671.65 = **1.62×**; ROE = 6.87% × 1.21 × 1.62 = **13.5%**
(matches the reference 13.5%) (3). Five-factor: tax burden 90.5 ÷ 121.0 = **0.748**; interest burden 121.0 ÷ 138.0 =
**0.877**; EBIT margin 138.0 ÷ 1,318 = **10.5%** (3).

**B2 (8).** (a) Incremental ROIC = (100.35 − 91.37) ÷ (886.8 − 690.6) = 8.98 ÷ 196.2 = **4.6%** (3). (b) Working capital
rose from ₹193.7 Cr to ₹365.0 Cr: ₹171.3 Cr, **87%** of the increase in invested capital (2). (c) The average ROIC (12.0%)
hides that new capital earned under 5% — well below a ~12% WACC. Almost all the new capital went into receivables and
inventory (mostly the solar receivables from state agencies), so growth since FY24 has destroyed value unless those
receivables are collected ([04.3](../../04-financial-analysis/03-returns-on-capital.md)) (3).

**B3 (6).** Revenue ₹2,000 → ₹2,320: **+16.0%** (1). Year-1 average price = 2,000 ÷ 150 = ₹13.33. Volume effect: (160 −
150) × 13.33 = **+₹133.3** (1.5). Mix effect: year-2 units at year-1 prices (90 × 10 + 70 × 20 = 2,300) − 160 × 13.33 =
**+₹166.7** (1.5). Price effect: 2,320 − 2,300 = **+₹20** (1.5). Total 320 ✓ (0.5). Most of the growth was mix (towards
the dearer product B), not price.

**B4 (4).** EBIT = 500 × 0.45 − 150 = **₹75 Cr**; at ₹550 Cr revenue, 247.5 − 150 = **₹97.5 Cr** (+30%) (2). DOL = 30% ÷ 10% =
**3.0** (1). A 10% fall gives 202.5 − 150 = **₹52.5 Cr (−30%)** (1).

**B5 (6).** Accruals ratio (90.5 − 65.1) ÷ 1,089.7 = **2.3%** (1). FCF = 65.1 − 48.0 − 4.0 = **₹13.1 Cr**; FCF ÷ PAT =
**14.5%** (2). Net debt ÷ EBITDA = 140.5 ÷ 181.9 = **0.77×**; interest cover 134.1 ÷ 17.0 = **7.9×** (2). The worrying number
is **FCF ÷ PAT of 14.5%**: the balance sheet is comfortable, but the company converts less than a sixth of its profit into
free cash — the profit is going into working capital (1).

## Part C (25 marks)

**1 (5).** EBITDA: **196.8, 252.5, 273.7, 239.4, 253.8**. FCF: **−30, −90, −160, −90, +90; total −280**. CFO ÷ EBITDA: 76%,
67%, 58%, 79%, 95%. Net debt ÷ EBITDA: 0.61×, 1.15×, 1.90×, 2.67×, 2.40×. Revenue CAGR **14.5%**; EBITDA CAGR **6.6%** — growth
bought with margin.

**2 (5).** EBITDA rose ₹57.0 Cr (196.8 → 253.8) on cumulative capex of **₹1,190 Cr**: about **₹0.05 of EBITDA per rupee
of capex**, i.e. a pre-tax return near 5% on the expansion so far. Some of that is under-utilisation (45%) and may improve,
but the expansion has so far earned far below the cost of capital while net debt rose fivefold.

**3 (6).** *Supported sources of advantage:* process know-how and patents (14 filings); multi-year contracts and two
take-or-pay agreements; customer qualification (switching costs are real in regulated agro and pharma supply chains).
*Threats:* concentration (top three 58%, one customer 31%) gives customers bargaining power; Chinese competition is
compressing prices, suggesting the product is closer to a commodity than the contracts imply; the industry-wide capacity
build (the capital cycle) plus Sahyadri's own 45%-utilised plant means price discipline is weak. On balance: a narrow,
contract-based advantage, not a wide moat.

**4 (5).** Capital allocation: aggressive, debt-funded expansion at the top of the cycle, with returns falling from 26%
to 12% ROCE; capex has since been cut (FY26 ₹150 Cr) — a sensible response. Governance: no pledge is a positive; the
promoter-group land lease is a related-party transaction to monitor (terms, escalation). Questions: (i) "What price and
utilisation assumptions give 22–24% margins, and what utilisation did the new plant reach in the latest quarter?"
(ii) "What share of FY27 revenue is under take-or-pay or fixed-price contracts, and when do the largest customer's
contracts come up for renewal?"

**5 (4).** **Medium–Low.** Deciding facts: (1) returns on the expansion (~5% on ₹1,190 Cr of capex) and ROCE falling to
12%; (2) customer concentration with a 31% customer; (3) margins falling with rising competition, while leverage peaked at
2.7× EBITDA. A recovery in utilisation could lift it to Medium; nothing in the data supports High.
