# Answer key — Mock 6 (Stock-pitch mock)

This key scores the two example pitches on the [rubric](../06-stock-pitch-mock.md#rubric) and gives model answers
to five questions from the question bank for Example A. Q&A (criterion 9) cannot be scored on a written pitch, so
both examples are scored out of 105 (21 weight × 5) and scaled to 100.

## Example A — "Priced for a 21% RoE, earning 15%"

| # | Criterion | Score | × Weight | Why |
|--:|:--|--:|--:|:--|
| 1 | Decision and clarity | 5 | 15 | Call, range, entry price and size all in the header |
| 2 | Expectations and variant perception | 5 | 15 | Implied RoE of 21% against a best-ever 15.9%; one precise variant claim |
| 3 | Evidence | 4 | 12 | Every point is a number with a source. No evidence the market lacks (for example, used-CV auction prices or dealer checks) |
| 4 | Business and moat | 2 | 4 | Almost silent on the franchise: branch economics in tier-3/4 towns, the underwriting edge, why borrowers choose Nirmal |
| 5 | Valuation | 4 | 12 | Right method (P/B–RoE) and three scenarios. Two gaps: the bear case assumes the same 13% book growth, when losses would slow it, and there is no sensitivity to kₑ |
| 6 | Risks and thesis-breakers | 4 | 12 | A specific pre-mortem and dated breakers. Breaker 3 is vague ("without yield cuts" — by how much?) |
| 7 | Accounting and governance | 3 | 6 | Calls accounting Green without showing the work. It should address stage-2 loans, write-offs (derived ₹57 Cr → ₹85 Cr) and the ECL model's assumptions. The PE overhang is well spotted |
| 8 | Sizing | 4 | 4 | Size derived from the max-loss rule and staged. Not checked against quarter-Kelly: with a −80% tail at 10%, it is 8.0%, so the 6.8% max-loss target binds, which is correct |
| 10 | Communication | 5 | 5 | Conclusion first, tight, no filler |
| | **Total** | | **85 / 105 → 81** | |

**How to lift it to 90+:**

- Add one piece of primary evidence, such as used-CV resale prices from auction platforms or two dealer
  conversations on repossessions.
- Show the kₑ sensitivity.
- Slow book growth in the bear case.
- Put the stage-2 trend in thesis point 1.

**The instructive part.** Every *fact* in Example B is true. The pitch fails on what it leaves out and on the
method. It anchors on management's framing, compares against the wrong peers, uses a multiple that ignores returns,
and never asks what the price already assumes. Most bad pitches look like this: accurate, confident and useless.

## Model Q&A answers for Example A

**Q1 — What does the price imply, and why is the market wrong?** At 2.61× book, with kₑ at 13% and g at 8%,
implied RoE = 8% + 2.61 × 5% = **21%**. The market is extrapolating 20% AUM growth and assuming returns rise. I think
returns fall first, because stage-3 loans are compounding faster than the book (+71% against +47% over two years).

**Q2 — Most sensitive assumption?** Sustainable RoE. Each 1 pp of RoE is 0.2× book, or about **₹35/share** on FY27E
book of ₹174. Next is kₑ: at 12% the base case is worth **₹348**, and at 14% **₹232**. The call survives any
kₑ from 12% to 14% unless sustainable RoE is above ~17%.

**Q10 — What does the best bear on your call (the bull) say?** "Nirmal compounds book at 13–15% while growing AUM
at 20%. The CV cycle is supported by five years of infrastructure spending. Tractors and MSME loans are 45% of the
book and diversify the cycle. Quality vehicle financiers have traded at 2.5–3.5× book for a decade." The fair
response is that the premium financiers earn 18–22% RoE; Nirmal earns 15%.

**Q16 — What would make you change your mind within three months?** A Q2 FY27 print with stage-3 below 2.7%,
credit cost below 1.6% and used-CV disbursements still growing. That would say the uptick was a blip, and I would
move to Watch-Buy at ₹330.

**Q25 — Why hasn't someone smarter arbitraged this away?** They may have, partly; the stock is 4% below its March
2025 price of ₹420. The edge is small and rests on three things:

- the stage-3 inflection is in the annual-report notes, not the headline presentation;
- the market anchors on AUM growth;
- the PE overhang is a supply technical that fundamentals-only investors ignore.

If I am wrong about the cycle, I lose nothing, because I own nothing at ₹402.
