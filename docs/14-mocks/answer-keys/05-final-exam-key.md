## Section B (30)

**B1 (8).**

(a) (3 marks) Ind AS 115 treats consideration payable to a customer — here, schemes paid to distributors and
dealers — as a **reduction of revenue**. The exception is payment for a **distinct** good or service the customer
provides, at fair value.

- *For the company:* some schemes pay for real, separable services, such as in-store display or co-op advertising.
- *Against:* volume-linked and sell-through schemes are price discounts. They are not distinct services, and the
  KAM flags exactly this estimate.

Presenting all ₹62 Cr as A&P is aggressive at best. FY24 is also not restated, which breaks comparability.

(b) (3 marks) Revenue on the old basis:

| | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Revenue | 1,760 | 1,932 | 2,148 |
| Growth | 9.3% | **9.8%** (reported 12.5%) | **11.2%** (reported 11.6%) |
| Gross margin | 37.0% | **36.6%** | **36.7%** |

(c) (2 marks) The "150 bp expansion" is **entirely presentation**. On a like-for-like basis the gross margin fell
about 30 bp over two years, despite the motor plant.

**B2 (6).**

- Adjusted EBITDA = 256 − 15 = **₹241 Cr**, a 11.2% margin on adjusted revenue against 11.6% in FY25, so the margin
  *fell*.
- Adjusted PAT = 138.4 − 15 × (1 − 0.2517) = **₹127.2 Cr**. That is growth of **+1.8%**, against a reported +10.7%.
- Warranty provision as a share of revenue: **1.48%** (FY24), **1.46%** (FY25), **0.77%** (FY26).

The question to ask: "Motors carry a five-year warranty, and FY25–FY26 sales include motors from a new plant and a
growing share from Kabini Motors. How many years of claims on these motors support a release? What would the
provision be at the FY21–FY25 claims ratio?"

**B3 (6).** The pattern is **channel stuffing / loading the channel**: pushing stock to distributors on extended
credit to meet revenue targets.

Evidence (any three):

- receivable days rose from 42 to 66 while revenue growth was modest;
- the Q4 share of revenue rose from 27% to 29% to 32%;
- credit terms were "extended selectively";
- CFO ÷ EBITDA fell to 36%;
- growth in trade schemes, now hidden in A&P, is consistent with incentives to take stock.

Confirm or refute (any two):

- the **receivables ageing schedule** (Schedule III), including dealer receivables over 180 days;
- the **Q1 FY27 revenue and sales returns**, since stuffed quarters are followed by weak ones;
- **channel checks** on distributor inventory weeks;
- **credit notes issued after year-end**;
- growth in the **allowance for expected credit loss**.

**B4 (6).**

- Related-party motor purchases as a share of cost of materials: **8.0%** (FY24), **11.6%** (FY25), **14.0%** (FY26).
- The own motor plant opened in FY25 and runs at 40% utilisation. Yet purchases from the family company *more than
  doubled*. The listed company is paying for capacity it leaves idle while buying from the promoters.

Questions for the audit committee (any three):

1. What is the price per motor from Kabini Motors against the internal cost at the new plant and against quotes
   from unrelated suppliers?
2. Why are volumes rising while the own plant is under-utilised, and what is the plan to shift volumes?
3. What are Kabini Motors' revenue, margins and other customers? Is Kabini its main customer?
4. Who on the committee reviewed the arm's-length test, and was an external benchmark used?

**B5 (4).** **Amber**, leaning Red. Accept Red if it is argued. The deciding items:

1. revenue presentation that flatters growth and gross margin, together with an opportunistic warranty release;
2. deteriorating cash conversion with signs of channel stuffing;
3. rising related-party purchases alongside an idle own plant, plus the CFO's resignation in the same year.

Mitigants: an unmodified audit opinion, no pledge, low leverage.

## Section C (20)

**C1 (8).**

- *Sources of advantage:* a regional brand with #2–#3 positions in mature categories, and an 18,000-outlet
  distribution network built over decades. The advantage is narrow and regional (44% of revenue from the South).
- *Erosion:*
  - E-commerce and quick commerce (18% of sales, doubling) weaken distribution as a moat. Shelf space online is
    rented, not owned, and price transparency compresses margins.
  - Small appliances are outsourced from contract manufacturers — no manufacturing edge, and exposed to
    white-label and D2C brands.
  - "Heightened competitive intensity" and rising schemes show that pricing power is weakening.

*Verdict:* a modest brand-plus-distribution moat that is narrowing
([05.3](../../05-business-analysis/03-moats-and-competitive-advantage.md)).

**C2 (6).**

- Capital employed rose from ₹472.0 Cr to ₹892.0 Cr, an increase of ₹420 Cr.
- Incremental pre-tax ROCE = (201 − 118) ÷ 420 = **19.8%**. Excluding the warranty release it is (186 − 118) ÷ 420 =
  **16.2%**. Both are below the 24.7% average, so new capital earns less than the old.
- Net working capital rose from ₹148 Cr to ₹386 Cr, an increase of ₹238 Cr (**57%** of the capital increase).
  Fixed assets rose by ₹182 Cr (43%).

Growth is being bought with dealer credit and a plant running at 40%.

**C3 (6).** Weak.

- Capex of ₹325 Cr went into a motor plant that sits under-utilised while the promoter's company supplies more
  motors each year.
- Dividends (₹138 Cr) were paid out of borrowing while FCF was negative.
- Reported numbers were made to look better through presentation and estimate changes.

Answer: **no**, not without evidence that incremental returns and related-party purchasing are fixed.

**D4 (6).** To justify ₹360:

- (a) a steady-state EBITDA margin of **14.9%** (against 13.0% in the base case and a like-for-like 11.2% today);
- (b) constant ten-year revenue growth of **14.7%** (against ~10% like-for-like history);
- (c) a WACC of **10.7%**.

The most plausible route is a **lower discount rate plus some margin expansion**. Indian consumer franchises
routinely trade on low-10s implied discount rates. Margin expansion of ~370 bp from the adjusted 11.2%, while
competition intensifies, and 15% growth for a decade, are both demanding. The price embeds "consumer compounder"
expectations that the accounts do not currently support
([06.6](../../06-valuation/06-reverse-dcf-and-expectations.md)).
