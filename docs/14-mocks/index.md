# Module 14 · Mocks & Drills

**What this module does.** It tests the course under exam conditions. There are four topic mocks, a four-hour final
exam on a new company, a stock-pitch mock with a rubric and question bank, and two drills to repeat until they are
fast. Every mock has a worked answer key with marks.

**Why it matters.** Reading about analysis feels like learning; doing it against a clock shows what you actually
know. Traders will recognise the principle: you do not know your edge until you have been scored on it. The mocks
are also where the pieces from different modules have to fit together — accounting feeding ratios, ratios feeding
valuation, forensics adjusting both.

## How to take a mock

1. **Sit it timed and closed-book.** Use a calculator; use a blank spreadsheet only where the paper allows it. Open
   the key only when the time is up.
2. **Mark it strictly.** Award method marks as the key describes, but a wrong number is wrong. For written answers,
   compare your reasoning with the key's; if yours differs but is defensible, write down why.
3. **Keep an error log.** For every miss, record:
   - the question;
   - the kind of error — *concept* (didn't know), *method* (knew it, applied it wrongly), *arithmetic*, *reading*
     (misread the question) or *judgement* (a defensible answer, just not the best one);
   - the lesson to re-read.
4. **Re-sit the misses a week later.** A concept error that repeats is a module to redo, not a question to memorise.
5. **Use the thresholds in each key.** Each key says what to do at 70%+, 55–69% and below.

!!! tip "What the mocks do not test"
    Judgement under real uncertainty, with no answer key. The final exam and the pitch mock come closest. The
    [capstone](../15-capstone/index.md) is where it happens for real, on companies you choose, with outcomes you
    will learn about a year later.
