# Drill — speed ratios & mental maths

> **Why this drill:** in a meeting, on a call or reading a results release, you rarely have time to open a
> spreadsheet. Analysts who can compute a margin, a CAGR, an implied multiple or a dilution in their heads catch
> errors and spot opportunities in real time. The arithmetic is simple; speed and accuracy come from practice.

**Take:** any time; repeat monthly. **Format:** four rounds of eleven questions, **6 minutes per round**, no calculator
(pen and paper allowed). Score one mark per answer within 2% (or the stated rounding) of the key. Target: 36/44 in
under 24 minutes. Record your score and time in your learning journal and retake the rounds you miss.

!!! tip "Tools you will want in your head"
    Rule of 72 (doubling time ≈ 72 ÷ rate); $1.1^{n}$ for n = 2, 3, 5, 7, 10 (1.21, 1.33, 1.61, 1.95, 2.59); $1.15^{5} ≈ 2.0$;
    $1.2^{5} ≈ 2.5$; a fall of *x* needs a rise of *x*/(1 − *x*) to recover; earnings yield = 1/P/E; value of a growing
    perpetuity = CF₁/(r − g); duration ≈ 1/(r − g); P/B = P/E × RoE.

## Round A — margins, percentages and per-share (6 minutes)

1. Revenue ₹1,250 Cr, EBITDA ₹175 Cr. EBITDA margin?
2. Gross margin 38%, opex 24% of sales, D&A 4% of sales. EBIT margin?
3. Sales rise 10%; costs (80% of sales) rise 8%. EBIT growth from a 20% margin base?
4. Fixed costs 30, variable costs 50% of sales. Sales go from 100 to 110. EBIT change?
5. Price +5%, volume −2%. Revenue change?
6. A 20% fall followed by a 20% rise. Net change?
7. A stock falls 60%. What rise gets it back?
8. PAT ₹90.5 Cr on 6.0 Cr shares. EPS?
9. A 10% position falls 50%. Portfolio impact?
10. ₹25 lakh as a percentage of ₹1 crore?
11. ₹2,340 crore in US$ million at ₹88 to the dollar?

## Round B — growth and compounding (6 minutes)

12. Rule of 72: how long does money take to double at 12%?
13. ₹100 becomes ₹250 in five years. CAGR?
14. 15% a year for ten years. Multiple?
15. Revenue ₹612 Cr → ₹1,318 Cr in five years. CAGR?
16. 20% a year for three years. Multiple?
17. EPS grows 12% a year for five years while the P/E falls from 40× to 30×. Annual price return?
18. Discount ₹1,000 due in five years at 12%.
19. ₹1 lakh crore in ₹ billion?
20. Ten crore rupees in millions of rupees?
21. Expected return: 25% chance of −35%, 50% of +23%, 25% of +60%.
22. Kelly fraction for a bet that wins 1:1 with 60% probability?

## Round D — perpetuities, dilution and portfolios (6 minutes)

34. A perpetuity of ₹10 a year at 10%. Value?
35. Growing perpetuity: next cash flow ₹10, r 12%, g 5%. Value?
36. Duration of that perpetuity?
37. If r rises from 12% to 13% (g 5%), by how much does its value change?
38. Terminal value: next year's FCF ₹243 Cr, WACC 12.19%, g 5.5%.
39. A company with 100 Cr shares issues 10 Cr new shares. Dilution of existing holders?
40. QIP of ₹300 Cr at ₹300 a share; 10 Cr shares before. New share count and dilution?
41. 1 Cr options with strike ₹200; share price ₹400. Treasury-stock-method dilution in shares?
42. Buyback of 5% of shares at 20× earnings, funded with cash earning 7% pre-tax (tax 25%). EPS accretion or dilution?
43. Rights issue 1-for-4 at ₹200; share price ₹300. Theoretical ex-rights price?
