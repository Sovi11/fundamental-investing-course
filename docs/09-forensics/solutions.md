# Module 09 · Solutions

## Warm-up

**E09.01** Conservative (understated profit; adjust up, watch for released cushions); neutral (use as is);
aggressive (overstated; adjust down, weight the bear case); manipulated (target-seeking; heavy discount or avoid);
fraudulent (fiction; avoid — equity usually worthless).

**E09.02** Pressure (guidance misses, pledges, fund-raising, covenants, bonus metrics); opportunity (promoter
control, ornamental audit committee, small auditor, complex structures); rationalisation (not observable —
inferred from disclosure tone and "temporary" language).

**E09.03** Earnings manipulation (premature/bogus revenue; capitalising costs; under-provisioning; cookie jars;
big baths); cash-flow manipulation (financing in CFO — supplier finance, factoring; operating outflows in CFI;
one-off boosts); key-metric manipulation (adjusted EBITDA; redefined contribution margins; GMV as revenue;
dropped KPIs).

**E09.04** Employees/whistle-blowers, media, analysts and short sellers detect most cases; auditors a minority.
An unmodified opinion is necessary, not sufficient; external scepticism and the numbers themselves are the
earlier signals.

**E09.05** See [09.2](02-revenue-red-flags.md), [09.3](03-expense-and-asset-red-flags.md), [09.4](04-cash-flow-games.md)
key-terms tables.

**E09.13** Closing = 80 + 12 − 15 − 30 = **₹47 Cr**. The ₹30 Cr reversal went to profit — 37% of the opening balance;

**E09.14** Implied yield = 28/1,650 = **1.7%** vs 6–7% market; interest paid ≈ ₹108 Cr on ₹1,200 Cr of debt. The
company pays ~9% on borrowings while "holding" cash earning 1.7%: window-dressed, restricted or non-existent cash

**E09.15** Reclassify the programme as debt: net debt 600 + 900 = ₹1,500 Cr; net debt/EBITDA from 0.5x to

**E09.16** The parent generates cash and lends it to subsidiaries that lose money; consolidated cash may sit in
entities the parent cannot upstream or may be transient. Value the group on the subsidiaries' economics (loss-
making) and treat the ₹520 Cr of loans as impaired risk; the "₹640 Cr of cash" is not the parent's.

## Stretch

**E09.24** Rubric: the scheme (e.g., fake distributors buying on 180-day credit; goods "shipped" to a rented
warehouse) and its marks — P&L: revenue and gross margin above peers; balance sheet: receivables and inventory
rising, cash flat; CFS: CFO ≪ PAT; notes: new large customers, extended terms, related warehouse lessor; tests:
DSO/ageing, customer verification, GST e-way-bill volumes vs shipments, dealer checks.

**E09.25** Both models take reported inputs; fabricated cash and revenue kept Altman's liquidity and Beneish's
indices normal. The catches: interest income vs cash (yield far below market) and the tax paid vs reported profit
(and, in Satyam's case, the attempted Maytas acquisition as a use for cash that did not exist).

**E09.26** Rubric: criteria coded (pledge > 20%; 3-yr CFO/PAT < 50%; auditor resignation; ASM/GSM; RPT > 10% of
costs), applied to a Screener export; a defensible estimate (typically 15–30% of a small-cap universe) with the
count by criterion.
