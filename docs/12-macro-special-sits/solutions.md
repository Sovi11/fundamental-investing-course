## Core

**E12.06** $k_e$ = 7.07 + 1.05 × 6.0 = 13.37%; $k_d$ post-tax = (7.07 + 2.4) × 0.7483 = 7.09%; WACC = 0.9 × 13.37 + 0.1 ×

**E12.07** Rubric: bank (rates/NIM, credit cycle, deposit competition), IT (INR +, rates duration −, AI), FMCG
(inputs, rural demand, GST), cement (capex, fuel, regional pricing), pump maker (monsoon, copper, state
finances/capex, flows). Dominant shared exposures: domestic flows (all five are DII-favoured), rates duration
(IT/FMCG/small cap), and government capex/state finances (cement/pumps).

**E12.08** Bank: mild negative (imported inflation → no cuts; credit costs if rural stress); IT: positive (INR);
FMCG: negative (packaging/crude derivatives, rural demand); cement: negative (fuel and freight, imported petcoke);
pump maker: negative (copper in ₹ up, rural demand down, state finances strained) — with the note that Kaveri's
industrial segment is less exposed.

**E12.09** Earnings yield 1/19.5 = 5.1%; gap = 5.1 − 7.07 = **−2.0 pp**. "Cheap" only relative to the market's own
5-year median (~22x); not cheap vs bonds (a negative gap) and possibly not cheap vs normalised earnings if FY25–26
margins are a peak. The reading is midpoint-to-optimistic in valuation, with earnings risk.

equity flows and SIP stoppage ratio; NSDL FPI flows; SEBI primary-market issuance; DII share of ownership.

**E12.13** Parent pre-demerger equity = 8,000; division equity value = 1,500 − 600 = 900 → per spun share (5 Cr
shares) = **₹180**; retained parent value = 8,000 − 900 = 7,100 → **₹710** per parent share. Holder of 2 shares:
2 × 710 + 180 = 1,600 = 2 × 800 ✓. Forced selling: index funds and large-cap mandates sell the small spun entity
(₹900 Cr) on listing; the spun stock often trades below ₹180 for weeks, which is the opportunity if the division's
economics justify the value.

**E12.14** Accepted 42 shares × (1,500 − 1,250) = ₹10,500 pre-tax gain (8.4% of the ₹1.5 lakh holding). Tax at 30% on
proceeds 42 × 1,500 = 63,000 → ₹18,900 → **net −₹8,400** before the capital-loss offset (42 × 1,250 = ₹52,500 of loss
usable against other gains, worth up to ₹6,563 at 12.5% or ₹10,500 at 20%). The tender is marginal-to-negative for a
30%-slab investor.

**E12.15** Spread = 420/395 − 1 = 6.3%; annualised (8 weeks) ≈ 41% simple, ~49% compounded. Acceptance if 60% tendered: 26/60 = **43%** of
tendered shares accepted; the rest stay with the holder at the post-offer price — the expected return depends on
that price, not only the spread.

**E12.16** TERP = (4 × 300 + 200)/5 = **₹280**; RE value = 280 − 200 = **₹80**. A holder who neither subscribes nor
sells the RE loses ₹80 × entitlement — value transferred to those who subscribe.

**E12.17** Value if completed: 0.5 × 210 = ₹105; EV = 0.85 × 105 + 0.15 × 70 = 89.25 + 10.5 = **₹99.75** vs price ₹95:
expected return 5% in six months (~10% annualised) with a 26% loss if it fails — a poor asymmetry unless the
completion probability is higher than 85%.

**E12.18** Forced selling of ~8% of float in the days around the effective date; the price typically falls ahead of
and into the event, then recovers if fundamentals are intact. Check: why it was excluded (free-float/liquidity
mechanics vs a fundamental decline), the company's earnings trend, and whether a re-inclusion is plausible.

**E12.19** Holdco at 0.45 × NAV; merger at 0.80 × NAV → return = 0.80/0.45 − 1 = **+78%**. Probability-weighted:
0.7 × 78% + 0.3 × 0% = **+54%** expected — with the caveat that a failed deal can widen the discount and that the
holder becomes an operating-company shareholder at 0.8 × NAV.

## Stretch

**E12.20** Rubric: flows depend on household behaviour (income growth, returns experience, alternatives such as
deposit rates and gold), which has not been tested by a multi-year drawdown; monitoring: SIP stoppage ratio,
net equity MF flows, redemptions in small-cap funds, retail F&O activity, deposit-rate competition, and the
ownership share of DIIs vs FPIs.

**E12.21** Rubric: primary document (RHP) read; arithmetic (valuation per share vs global exchanges and BSE/MCX;
implied volumes and fee path); forced actors (anchor lock-ins, retail allotment flips, index inclusion timing);
independent valuation ([07.2](../07-special-valuation/02-insurers-amcs-exchanges.md)); breakers (SEBI fee action,
derivative-volume decline, governance legacy issues); sizing as an event with a dated exit or as an investment
at a supported price.

**E12.22** For: base rates say forward returns from top-decile valuations are poor, and cash is the option to buy
lower. Against: bottom-up opportunities exist in any market, timing is unreliable, and cash has a carry cost; the
rule that works is to let the *margin of safety* on individual names set the cash level (no triggers in reach →
cash rises automatically) rather than an index-level switch.
