# Module 12 · Exercises

## Warm-up

**E12.01** Derive equity duration from the Gordon model and compute it for g = 4%, 6%, 8% at r = 12%.

**E12.02** List the channels from a repo-rate change to equity prices.

**E12.03** State the RBI's policy rate, stance and the latest CPI as of September 2026, and where you would verify
them.

**E12.04** Define: earnings-yield gap; market cap/GDP; India VIX; SIP stoppage ratio; grey-market premium.

**E12.05** Define: scheme of arrangement; record date; tender-offer buyback; acceptance ratio; open offer;
reverse book building; rights entitlement; swap ratio.

## Core

**E12.06** Kaveri's reference WACC uses rf 6.5%. Rebuild it at 7.07% and compute the value change from the
sensitivity grid; express it as duration.

**E12.07** Fill the sector × macro matrix for a five-stock portfolio: a private bank, an IT major, an FMCG leader, a
cement company and a small-cap pump maker. Identify the dominant shared exposure.

**E12.08** Crude rises 25% and the rupee weakens 6%. Write the first-order P&L direction and magnitude driver for
each of the five holdings in E12.07.

**E12.09** The Nifty trades at 19.5x trailing with EPS growth slowing to single digits; the G-sec yields 7.07%.
Compute the earnings yield and the gap, and write three sentences on whether the market is "cheap".

**E12.10** DIIs invested ₹8.5 lakh Cr in FY26 while FPIs sold ₹1.8 lakh Cr. Describe two ways this regime supports
valuations and two ways it is a risk, and the data you would watch monthly.

**E12.11** Place these markers on the primary-market cycle: (a) an SME IPO subscribed 400x; (b) a large exchange IPO
subscribed 5.7x with QIBs at 12.7x; (c) three IPOs listing below issue price in a month; (d) OFS-heavy issues by PE
sellers. Which stage does each suggest?

**E12.12** For the 2013–18 and 2020–25 small-cap cycles, list the narrative, the flow, the supply response and the
governance tail, and the behaviour that survived.

**E12.13** *Demerger.* A company at ₹800 with 10 Cr shares demerges a division valued by you at ₹1,500 Cr (1:2 ratio —
one new share per two held); debt of ₹600 Cr moves with the division. Compute the value per parent share retained
and per spun share, and describe the forced-selling dynamic.

**E12.14** *Buyback.* Tender at ₹1,500 for 4% of shares; stock ₹1,250; small-shareholder acceptance 35%; you hold 120
shares (under the ₹2 lakh cap). Compute the pre-tax gain and the post-tax result at a 30% slab on proceeds treated
as dividend (ignore the capital-loss offset).

**E12.15** *Open offer.* An acquirer buys 30% at ₹420 and must offer 26% to the public; the stock trades at ₹395 with
the offer closing in 8 weeks. Compute the spread, the annualised return if fully accepted, and the acceptance
ratio if 60% of public shares are tendered.

**E12.16** *Rights.* A 1:4 rights issue at ₹200 with the stock at ₹300. Compute the theoretical ex-rights price and
the RE value; explain what happens to a holder who does nothing.

**E12.17** *Merger arbitrage.* Target trades at ₹95; swap 1 acquirer share (₹210) for 2 target shares; completion in 6
months with 85% probability, and ₹70 if it fails. Compute the expected value and the implied return.

**E12.18** *Index event.* A stock is announced for exclusion from a major index with passive holders at 8% of its
float. Describe the expected price path and the opportunity, with the check you would run on fundamentals.

**E12.19** *Holdco event.* A holdco at a 55% discount announces a merger into its operating company at NAV less 20%.
Compute the return for a holdco holder and the probability-weighted return if the deal has a 70% chance of
completing within a year (else the discount stays 55%).

## Stretch

**E12.20** Explain why domestic-flow-supported valuations are a "regime, not a law", and design the monitoring
that would tell you the regime is changing.

**E12.21** Write the NSE IPO through the six-step special-situation checklist, using the RHP's structure as you
would expect it (or the actual document if available).

**E12.22** Argue whether a bottom-up investor should hold more cash when the Nifty's P/E is above its 90th
percentile; give the strongest case for each side and your rule.

## Real-world task

**E12.23** Build the macro audit for your actual portfolio (or a model one): the matrix, the dominant exposure,
and a hedge you would consider ([11.7](../11-process/07-fundamentals-meets-derivatives.md)).

**E12.24** Read one 2025–26 demerger scheme document (ITC Hotels, Tata Motors CV, HUL ice cream, Vedanta) end to
end; list the ratio, dates, liabilities moved, promoter holdings before/after, tax treatment, and the forced
sellers — and what the two entities did after listing.

**E12.25** Track the Nifty P/E, the 10-year G-sec, monthly FPI/DII flows and SIP data for the next three months in
a spreadsheet; note where the pendulum sits each month.
