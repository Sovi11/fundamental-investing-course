# 12.2 · Market cycles & sentiment

> **Why this matters:** the same company, with the same cash flows, has traded at 15x and 45x within five years
> because the *market's* mood changed. A fundamental investor cannot time the mood, but can measure it —
> index valuation bands, flows, IPO froth, small-cap premia — and adjust behaviour: more cash and tighter
> margins of safety when the pendulum is at one extreme, more aggression at the other. India's small-cap cycles
> (2008, 2018, 2024–25) are the reference, and September 2026 — Nifty below its median P/E, record domestic
> flows, FPIs at a 15-year-low ownership — is the live reading.

**Learning objectives** — after this lesson you can:

- Read index valuation measures (P/E and P/B bands, earnings yield vs bond yield, market cap/GDP) and state their
  limits.
- Track flows (FPI vs DII, SIPs) and primary-market activity (IPOs, QIPs) as sentiment gauges.
- Describe India's small-cap cycles and the behaviour that survived them.
- Apply Howard Marks's pendulum to behaviour: what to do at extremes, and what not to do in the middle.

**Prerequisites:** [12.1 Macro for equity investors](01-macro-for-equity-investors.md), [06.5 Multiples](../06-valuation/05-relative-valuation-and-multiples.md)  ·  **Time:** ~60 min

## 1. Measuring the market's mood

| Measure | How | Reading (Sep-2026) | Limits |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **Index trailing P/E vs its history** | Nifty 50 P/E vs 10–20-year bands (median, ±1σ) | ~19.5x vs a 5-year median of ~22x; historical band 2 of 5, from which 5-year forward returns have historically been positive with a median in the low teens ([IndexPE](https://indexpe.in/nifty-50); [PrimeInvestor](https://primeinvestor.in/nifty-pe-ratio/)) | Earnings at a cycle peak make P/E look low (FY22, FY25); index composition changes (consolidated EPS from 2021 lowered the P/E series — compare like with like) |
| **Index P/B** | Nifty P/B vs history (typically 2.5–4.5x) | Mid-range | ROE changes shift the fair band |
| **Earnings yield vs 10-year G-sec** | 1/PE vs yield | ~5.1% vs 7.07%: a negative gap — equities offer less current yield than bonds | India has almost always had a negative gap because of growth; use the *change* in the gap, not its sign |
| **Market cap / GDP** ("Buffett indicator") | Total mcap ÷ nominal GDP | Elevated vs history (India has listed more of its economy; new listings inflate it) | Structural drift; compare across countries with care |
| **Small-cap vs large-cap premium** | Nifty Smallcap 250 P/E ÷ Nifty 50 P/E | Above 1 for most of 2023–25; compressed in the 2025 correction; check current | Small-cap indices include loss-makers; use median P/E |
| **Breadth** | % of stocks above 200-day averages; advance/decline | — | Technical; useful as a sentiment thermometer, not a valuation |
| **Implied vol (India VIX)** | Level vs history | Low VIX at high valuations = complacency | Short-dated; regime indicator only |

None of these predicts the next year. Their use is to know *where in the range* the market sits, and hence how
much of any stock's multiple is the market's mood rather than its merits.

## 2. Flows

| Flow | Source | 2025–26 reading | Why it matters |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **FPI net equity flows** | NSDL/SEBI, daily | Net outflows of ~₹1.8 lakh Cr in FY26; selling persisted into 2026 ([Multibagg](https://www.multibagg.ai/market-pulse/articles/fii-fpi-india-2026-flows-cmoihcj2us18tp30j229mjvsc)); FPI ownership 15.8% of NSE-listed, a 15-year low | Historically the marginal buyer; now the marginal seller |
| **DII net flows (MFs, insurers, pension)** | SEBI/AMFI, monthly | Record ₹8.5 lakh Cr in FY26, of which mutual funds ₹6.4 lakh Cr; ownership 17% — a record ([SEBI annual report via OmmCom](https://ommcomnews.com/business-news/rs-8-5-lakh-crore-dii-inflows-dwarf-fpi-outflows-in-fy26-sebi/)); H1 2026 DII inflows ₹4.3 lakh Cr ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/dii-inflows-hit-record-4-000-crore-a-day-as-fpis-continue-to-exit-stocks-126061001079_1.html)) | The regime: domestic savings, not foreign capital, set prices |
| **SIP flows** | AMFI, monthly | ₹25,000+ Cr/month (verify current) | Sticky, but not permanent; SIP stoppage ratios rise in drawdowns |
| **Retail direct participation** | Demat accounts; NSE active clients; F&O volumes | Demat accounts > 20 crore (verify); F&O activity cut by SEBI's 2024–25 measures | Retail is now a structural holder and a sentiment gauge |
| **Primary market** | SEBI: IPOs, QIPs, OFS by month | 2024–25 record IPO volumes; NSE's ₹22,562 Cr IPO in Sep-2026 (5.7x subscribed) | Supply absorbs flows; a heavy IPO calendar at high valuations is a late-cycle marker |
| **Promoter selling** | SAST/bulk deals | Elevated in 2024–25 | Informed sellers meeting uninformed buyers |

The flow regime cuts two ways. Domestic flows have made Indian valuations resilient to FPI selling — the
2025–26 experience — and they have made the market dependent on a single behaviour (households' monthly SIP)
that has never been tested by a prolonged bear market. The base-rate question is not "will SIPs stop?" but "what
would the market look like if SIP inflows halved for a year?" — and the honest answer is that nobody knows,
because it has not happened.

## 3. IPO cycles and primary-market froth

Primary markets follow a recognisable sequence: quality issuers at reasonable prices → strong listings → a
rush of lower-quality issuers at higher prices → retail oversubscription and grey-market premia → listing-day
pops attracting more supply → a few high-profile failures → the window closes. Markers of the late stage:
subscription multiples in the hundreds; SME IPOs with tiny floats oversubscribed 500x (2023–24); IPOs priced on
"basis of offer price" arguments that reference no earnings; promoters selling most of the issue (OFS-heavy
deals); and post-listing underperformance across the cohort (2021's new-age tech — [case I8](../13-case-studies/india/08-zomato-paytm-ipos-2021.md)).
The rule from [03.5](../03-reading-filings/05-other-documents.md): read the DRHP, note the OFS share and the
pre-IPO placement prices, and treat listing-day trading as noise.

## 4. India's small-cap cycles

| Cycle | Run-up | Drawdown | What was said at the top | What survived |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| 2003–08 | Small caps up several-fold on the capex/credit boom | ~−70–75% peak to trough (2008–09) | "India decoupled"; infrastructure super-cycle | Companies with cash flow and no debt; investors with cash |
| 2013–18 | Small-cap index roughly tripled to Jan-2018 | ~−40–50% (2018–20), with many individual names −80%+ | "Quality at any price"; "formalisation" | Genuine compounders bought before 2016; the "quality" cohort de-rated but survived; the fraud cohort (Manpasand, Vakrangee, etc.) did not |
| 2020–24 | Post-COVID rally; small caps up ~4–5x from the March-2020 low; retail participation and SIPs | 2024–25 correction of ~−25–30% in the small-cap index with wider dispersion (verify magnitudes) | "Amrit kaal"; defence/rail/PSU narratives; SME mania | Names with earnings and governance; the narrative cohort de-rated |

Patterns: each cycle has a *narrative* that justifies the multiple, a *flow* that funds it, a *supply response*
(IPOs, QIPs, promoter selling) that meets the flow, and a *governance tail* that is exposed on the way down. The
survivors were bought with a margin of safety before the narrative took hold and were held through the drawdown
because the thesis, not the price, was the reason for holding.

## 5. The pendulum — and what to do about it

Howard Marks's image: markets swing between greed and fear, euphoria and depression, credulousness and
scepticism — and spend little time at the "fair" midpoint. The investor's job is to *notice where the pendulum
is* (the measures above), not to predict when it turns. Behaviour by position:

| Pendulum position | Signs | Behaviour |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **Euphoria** | Valuations at historical highs; IPO froth; "this time is different"; retail leverage; low VIX; every idea passes the kill test at a price you would not pay | Raise cash by refusing to buy without margin; trim positions above the bull case; tighten thesis-breakers; do not short (timing); keep researching — the watchlist is being built for later |
| **Optimism** | Above-median valuations; flows strong; earnings good | Normal process; smaller sizes; more attention to what the price implies |
| **Midpoint** | Median valuations; mixed flows | Bottom-up work dominates; the market is not the story |
| **Pessimism** | Below-median valuations; FPI selling; earnings downgrades; "value traps everywhere" | Deploy watchlist triggers; sizes toward the upper bound of the rules; expect to be early |
| **Depression** | Historical lows; forced selling; liquidity gone; "equities are dead" | Buy the best businesses at any size the rules allow; accept that the bottom cannot be identified except in hindsight |

Where is September 2026? Below-median index valuations on a slowing earnings cycle, record domestic flows,
FPI pessimism, a completed small-cap correction and a still-heavy primary calendar: *optimism in flows, pessimism
in earnings, midpoint in valuation* — a market where the bottom-up work decides, and where the flow regime is
the risk to name in every portfolio pre-mortem.

!!! tip "Trader's lens"
    Sentiment measures are the equity market's vol surface: level (valuation bands), skew (small-cap premium,
    IPO froth), and term structure (flow persistence). You already know that the surface tells you the price of
    risk, not its direction, and that the trade is regime-relative positioning — smaller when risk is cheap and
    everyone is long, larger when it is dear and nobody is. The same applies here; the only difference is the
    timescale, which is years, and the discipline, which has to survive being early.

!!! warning "Common mistakes"
    - Timing the market on valuation bands (they say "expensive", not "when").
    - Confusing a low index P/E at peak earnings with cheapness.
    - Treating domestic flows as permanent — or as about to reverse.
    - Selling everything in euphoria (missing the last, largest leg) or buying nothing in depression (missing the
      turn) — the rules are about sizes and margins, not all-or-nothing.
    - Reading sentiment from the same media that manufactures it.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|:--|:--|
| **Valuation band** | Historical range of an index multiple with median and dispersion |
| **Earnings yield gap** | Earnings yield minus the 10-year G-sec yield |
| **Market cap / GDP** | Total equity market value relative to the economy |
| **Breadth** | Share of stocks participating in a move |
| **India VIX** | Implied volatility index for the Nifty |
| **FPI / DII** | Foreign / domestic institutional investors |
| **SIP stoppage ratio** | SIPs discontinued as a share of new registrations |
| **Grey-market premium (GMP)** | Unofficial pre-listing price premium on an IPO |
| **Pendulum (Marks)** | The oscillation of market psychology between extremes |
| **Flow regime** | The prevailing marginal-buyer structure |

## Check your understanding

1. The Nifty trades at 19.5x trailing earnings, below its 5-year median. Give two reasons this may not be "cheap".
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Trailing earnings may be at a cycle peak (FY25–26 margins), so forward P/E on
normalised earnings is higher; and the earnings yield (~5.1%) is below the 7.07% G-sec yield, so equities are not
cheap relative to bonds — the "cheapness" is only relative to the market's own recent history.</details>

2. What would a halving of SIP flows for a year do, mechanically, and which holdings would feel it most?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Remove the marginal buyer that has absorbed FPI selling; small caps and
high-multiple, DII-heavy names would de-rate most; liquidity would thin. Test each holding's ownership (MF share)
and its multiple's dependence on the flow regime.</details>

3. A watchlist name hits your trigger price during a small-cap crash. The thesis is intact but liquidity is
   thin. What do the rules say?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Buy, within the liquidity limit and in tranches (expect to be early); do not
raise the size above the max-loss rule because "it's cheap"; the cash you held for this is the reason you held it.</details>

4. Name three late-cycle markers in the primary market.
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Subscription multiples in the hundreds (especially SME), OFS-heavy issues by
promoters and PE sellers, and pricing arguments that reference no earnings (GMV, TAM); post-listing
underperformance across a cohort confirms it.</details>
