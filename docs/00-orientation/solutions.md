# Module 00 · Solutions

## Warm-up

**E00.01** *Price*: what the last buyer paid. *Intrinsic value*: the present value of the cash the asset will
deliver to its owner, at a rate reflecting the risk. *Margin of safety*: the gap between price and estimated value,
judged relative to the uncertainty of the estimate. *Variant perception*: a well-founded belief that differs from
the expectations embedded in the price.

**E00.02** (1) What is the business? — business-model and industry analysis (Module 05). (2) Is it a good
business? — returns on capital, moats, governance (Modules 04–05, 09). (3) What is it worth versus its price? —
valuation and reverse DCF (Module 06).

**E00.03** Value (Graham; Klarman); quality/compounders (Buffett, Munger, Fisher); growth (Lynch; T. Rowe Price);
GARP (Lynch); special situations (Greenblatt); activism (Icahn; in India, Unifi/IiAS-style engagement); long/short
(Robertson, Steinhardt). Any four with a correct pairing.

**E00.04** Behavioural (investors over-react to news and extrapolate); structural/time-horizon (institutions
cannot hold through a two-year thesis; index rules force flows); informational (small companies are not covered);
analytical (the information is public but the work to interpret it — forensic, unit-economic — is not done).

**E00.05** Outcome vs process: a good outcome can follow a bad decision (luck) and a bad outcome a good one. Over
many decisions, process quality determines results; judging by outcomes alone teaches the wrong lessons.

## Core

**E00.06** (a) Sizing must survive long feedback delays and estimation error — fractional Kelly and max-loss
rules rather than edge-optimal sizing. (b) Skill cannot be read from a year's P&L; it must be judged from process
quality and calibration over many decisions (journals). (c) Written process (memos, thesis-breakers, checklists)
substitutes for the market's immediate feedback: it is how you find out you were wrong before the price tells you.
*Rubric: one concrete consequence per part; mention of feedback delay in (b); mention of pre-commitment in (c).*

**E00.07** A 25% target implies ~12 pp of annual alpha over the index; the base rate for individual investors
and most professional funds delivering sustained double-digit alpha is very low (SPIVA India reports show most
active large-cap funds trail the index over 5–10 years — verify current figures). Base rates: SPIVA India
scorecards, mutual-fund category returns (AMFI/Value Research), academic studies of retail returns. The claim
needs either an identified edge (small caps, special situations, a process) or a smaller target.

**E00.08** You would need the full population of stocks that *looked like* the winners at the time (same
approach, same signals) and their outcomes — including the ones that went to zero — to compute a hit rate and
expected return. The book shows only the survivors, selected after the fact; it cannot distinguish an edge from
selection.

**E00.09** Related-party note → "read filings" (Module 03; governance in 05.6). ROIC → "analyse the numbers"
(04.3). Implied growth → "compare with price/expectations" (06.6). How much to buy → "decide and size" (11.4).
Dealer inventory → "understand the business / scuttlebutt" (05.7).

## Stretch

**E00.13** *Transfers*: implied-vs-realised thinking (reverse DCF as implied growth); convexity (operating and
financial leverage as gamma; equity as a call on the firm); carry (lenders' spread; holdco discounts' negative
carry); regime awareness (valuation and flow regimes; the capital cycle); sizing by edge over variance; process
vs outcome. *Does not transfer*: immediate feedback and high sample counts (skill is not observable quickly);
hedgeability of the main risk (thesis risk is unhedgeable); the ability to scale in and out at will (liquidity in
small caps); and mean-reversion instincts on price (a stock down 50% is not "cheap vol" — the distribution may
have changed). *Rubric: three transfers and three non-transfers, each with a concrete link to a course concept.*
